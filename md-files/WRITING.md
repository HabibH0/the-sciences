# WRITING.md

## Purpose

Use this file whenever you write, rewrite, edit, summarize, explain,
document, or draft prose for this project.

The goal is not to make writing "sound human" by adding random quirks.
The goal is to produce writing that has a reason to exist: clear,
specific, informed by context, and free of the recurring patterns that
make generated prose feel generic.

These rules apply with judgment. Accuracy and meaning come before style.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Priority Order

When rules conflict, use this order:

1.  Be accurate.
2.  Preserve the intended meaning.
3.  Be clear.
4.  Be specific.
5.  Be concise enough for the task.
6.  Sound natural.
7.  Apply stylistic preferences.

Never distort a fact or remove necessary nuance merely to make a
sentence punchier.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Default Voice

Write directly and naturally.

Start with the substance. Do not spend the opening paragraph announcing
what you are about to say.

Prefer concrete language over abstract language.

Use active voice when it is natural, but do not mechanically eliminate
passive voice.

Vary sentence length and structure. A paragraph in which every sentence
has the same cadence sounds generated.

Short sentences are useful. So are longer ones when the idea needs them.

Use contractions in conversational prose when they fit.

Fragments are acceptable when deliberate. Not constantly.

Write as though a thoughtful person is communicating with another
person, not as though a content engine is filling a template.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Context Before Prose

Before writing, determine:

-   Who is this for?
-   What do they already know?
-   What do they need from this?
-   What is the actual point?
-   What facts, examples, constraints, or evidence are available?
-   What tone does the context require?
-   What should the reader think, understand, decide, or do afterward?

If source material exists, use its actual details rather than filling
gaps with generic prose.

Do not invent personal experiences, quotations, evidence, statistics,
sources, motivations, or opinions.

If important context is missing and cannot be inferred safely, ask for
it or make the uncertainty explicit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Specificity

Generic prose is usually weak prose.

Prefer:

-   actual nouns over vague categories;
-   concrete verbs over abstract action phrases;
-   exact examples over generic examples;
-   numbers over words like "significant" when the number is known;
-   named constraints over vague references to "challenges";
-   the actual mechanism over claims that something "helps" or
    "improves" something.

Instead of:

> This approach can significantly improve the user experience.

Say what improves and why:

> Keeping the draft in local storage means a page refresh does not erase
> the user's work.

Instead of:

> There are several important considerations.

Name the considerations.

Do not manufacture specificity when the facts are unknown.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Cut AI-Shaped Filler

Remove sentences that merely signal importance without adding
information.

Be suspicious of phrases such as:

-   "It's important to note that..."
-   "It's worth noting that..."
-   "It is crucial to understand..."
-   "In today's rapidly evolving..."
-   "In the ever-changing landscape of..."
-   "When it comes to..."
-   "At its core..."
-   "This highlights the importance of..."
-   "This underscores the need for..."
-   "This serves as a reminder that..."
-   "The key takeaway is..."
-   "Ultimately..."
-   "In conclusion..."
-   "As we have seen..."
-   "There are several factors to consider..."
-   "Whether you're a beginner or an expert..."
-   "The good news is..."
-   "Here's where things get interesting..."

These phrases are not absolutely forbidden. Use one only when it
genuinely earns its place.

Usually, delete the setup and state the point.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Avoid Inflated Language

Do not make ordinary things sound historic, transformative, profound, or
sweeping.

Avoid reflexive words such as:

-   transformative
-   revolutionary
-   groundbreaking
-   game-changing
-   cutting-edge
-   unprecedented
-   remarkable
-   powerful
-   robust
-   seamless
-   comprehensive
-   innovative
-   dynamic
-   vibrant
-   pivotal
-   crucial
-   invaluable
-   testament
-   landscape
-   realm
-   ecosystem
-   journey
-   tapestry
-   delve
-   leverage
-   unlock

These words are allowed when they are literally the best word. They
should not be default vocabulary.

Prefer describing what something does over praising it.

Bad:

> This powerful framework unlocks a seamless development experience.

Better:

> The framework handles authentication, routing, and database migrations
> in one project.

Let evidence create emphasis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Do Not Manufacture Profundity

Avoid dressing a simple observation up as a universal truth.

Do not turn every topic into:

-   a broader lesson about humanity;
-   a statement about "the future";
-   a cultural shift;
-   a philosophical contrast;
-   an inspirational conclusion.

If the point is practical, end practically.

Do not add a moral to an explanation that does not need one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Avoid Formulaic Contrast

AI writing overuses constructions like:

> It's not X. It's Y.

> This isn't just X; it's Y.

> Not only X, but Y.

> The question isn't X. The question is Y.

Use contrast when the distinction is genuinely useful. Do not use it as
a default way to manufacture emphasis.

Likewise, avoid repeatedly organizing prose into neat sets of three
merely because three items sound rhetorical.

Natural writing is not obliged to have a slogan every paragraph.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Sentence Rhythm

Do not produce a stream of sentences with identical length, grammar, or
cadence.

Watch for patterns like:

> It improves speed. It reduces complexity. It increases reliability. It
> enables scale.

Combine, vary, or prioritize them.

Avoid excessive one-line dramatic paragraphs.

Avoid making every paragraph end with a punchline.

Do not force choppy prose in an attempt to sound informal.

Read the passage for rhythm. It should feel written, not assembled.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Paragraphs

Each paragraph should have a job.

Start a new paragraph when the idea, stage, speaker, or emphasis
genuinely changes.

Do not split every sentence into its own paragraph.

Do not create enormous walls of text when several ideas need separation.

For ordinary explanatory writing, a mixture of short and medium
paragraphs is usually best.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 11. Headings

Use headings when they help navigation, not automatically.

Headings should describe the content beneath them.

Avoid vague headings such as:

-   "Overview"
-   "Key Insights"
-   "The Bigger Picture"
-   "Why It Matters"
-   "Looking Ahead"
-   "Final Thoughts"

when a more specific heading is available.

Do not turn a short answer into a miniature report with six headings.

Do not use title case everywhere unless the document's style requires
it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 12. Lists

Use a list when the reader genuinely benefits from scanning separate
items.

Do not turn ordinary prose into a list merely because lists are easy to
generate.

List items should be parallel enough to scan naturally.

Do not create a list whose items each contain several paragraphs unless
the structure genuinely requires it.

Avoid repetitive:

-   explanation;
-   "why it matters";
-   example;
-   takeaway;

for every item unless the task specifically calls for that format.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Punctuation

Use punctuation normally rather than stylistically.

### Em dashes

Do not use em dashes as a default sentence connector.

Prefer a comma, colon, semicolon, parentheses, or a new sentence when
one of those is more natural.

An occasional em dash is fine when it genuinely suits the sentence.
Repeated em dashes are a common generated-writing tell.

### Colons

Do not constantly introduce ordinary statements with dramatic colons.

### Parentheses

Use them for genuinely secondary information, not as a way to cram every
caveat into one sentence.

### Quotation marks

Do not put ordinary words in quotation marks for vague emphasis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Transitions

Do not force a transition at the beginning of every paragraph.

Avoid repetitive signposts such as:

-   Moreover,
-   Furthermore,
-   Additionally,
-   Consequently,
-   Importantly,
-   Interestingly,
-   Notably,
-   That said,
-   With that in mind,

when the relationship between sentences is already obvious.

A paragraph can simply begin with its point.

Use transitions when they clarify logic, not to make prose appear
polished.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Claims and Evidence

Match confidence to evidence.

Distinguish among:

-   fact;
-   inference;
-   estimate;
-   opinion;
-   recommendation;
-   speculation.

Do not quietly convert one into another.

If a claim is supported by a source, represent the source faithfully.

Do not use a citation as decoration for a stronger claim than the source
actually supports.

Prefer primary sources where appropriate.

Never invent a citation.

If evidence is mixed, say so.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. Explanations

When explaining something technical:

1.  Answer the actual question early.
2.  Establish the minimum concepts needed.
3.  Explain the mechanism.
4.  Give a concrete example if it helps.
5.  Address the likely source of confusion.
6.  Stop when the explanation is complete.

Do not bury the answer beneath background material.

Use terminology when it makes the explanation more precise. Define
unfamiliar terminology instead of replacing every technical word with an
imprecise analogy.

Analogies should illuminate the mechanism, not substitute for it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Academic and Technical Writing

For formal or academic prose:

-   prioritize precision over conversational flair;
-   define terms before relying on them;
-   state assumptions;
-   distinguish results from interpretation;
-   preserve qualifications and uncertainty;
-   use mathematical/statistical terminology correctly;
-   avoid promotional adjectives;
-   avoid pretending a result establishes more than it does.

Do not use contractions if the surrounding document is conventionally
formal and does not use them.

Do not "humanize" technical writing by making it less precise.

Prefer:

> The model's test accuracy increased from 81.2% to 84.7%.

over:

> The model delivered a remarkable improvement in performance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 18. Documentation

Documentation should help someone accomplish a task or understand the
system.

Prefer:

-   exact commands;
-   exact paths;
-   concrete examples;
-   prerequisites;
-   expected behaviour;
-   known limitations;
-   troubleshooting tied to actual failure modes.

Do not pad documentation with marketing copy.

Do not claim something is "easy," "simple," or "seamless." Show the
steps and let the reader decide.

Keep terminology consistent with the codebase.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 19. Emails and Messages

For email and conversational writing:

-   make the purpose clear early;
-   sound like a person, not a letter template;
-   include enough context for the recipient to act;
-   keep politeness natural;
-   make requests explicit;
-   avoid excessive gratitude, deference, or enthusiasm.

Avoid openings such as:

> I hope this email finds you well.

unless the user actually prefers them.

Avoid endings that repeat the entire request.

Match the level of formality to the relationship and context.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 20. Persuasive Writing

Persuasion should come from a clear argument, evidence, stakes, and
useful framing.

Do not manufacture persuasion through:

-   hype;
-   fake urgency;
-   exaggerated certainty;
-   empty superlatives;
-   rhetorical questions every few paragraphs;
-   excessive social-proof language;
-   invented anecdotes.

State the strongest real reason.

Acknowledge meaningful tradeoffs. Writing becomes more credible when it
does not pretend every choice is obvious.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 21. Do Not Invent a Personality

Never add fake autobiographical details such as:

> I've spent years seeing this problem...

unless the speaker actually supplied that experience.

Do not fabricate:

-   personal stories;
-   emotional reactions;
-   conversations;
-   preferences;
-   professional experience;
-   first-hand testing.

When writing in the user's voice, use only voice characteristics and
experiences supported by their material or instructions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 22. Preserve the User's Voice

When rewriting user-provided prose, edit rather than replace by default.

Preserve:

-   the user's actual argument;
-   distinctive vocabulary that works;
-   degree of formality;
-   technical depth;
-   personality;
-   intentional rhetorical choices.

Fix:

-   ambiguity;
-   repetition;
-   unnecessary filler;
-   awkward syntax;
-   weak organization;
-   unsupported claims;
-   accidental AI-like patterns.

Do not homogenize everything into generic professional English.

If the original has character, keep it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 23. Anti-Slop Editing Pass

After drafting substantial prose, perform a separate editing pass.

Ask:

### Substance

-   Does every paragraph add information, reasoning, evidence, or
    necessary connective tissue?
-   Did I say anything merely because it sounds good?
-   Are there vague claims that should be concrete?
-   Did I invent anything?

### Language

-   Are there stock AI phrases?
-   Are adjectives doing work that facts should do?
-   Are abstract nouns hiding a simpler verb?
-   Can any sentence lose words without losing meaning?
-   Did I repeat the same point in different language?

### Structure

-   Is the answer visible early enough?
-   Are headings and lists actually useful?
-   Is the structure suspiciously symmetrical or formulaic?
-   Did I add an unnecessary introduction or conclusion?

### Rhythm

-   Are too many sentences the same length?
-   Are there too many fragments?
-   Are there too many dramatic one-line paragraphs?
-   Are em dashes, colons, rhetorical questions, or contrast formulas
    repeating?

### Voice

-   Does this sound appropriate for the speaker and audience?
-   Did I preserve the user's own voice where one was available?
-   Does any sentence sound like generic corporate copy?

Revise anything that fails this pass.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 24. Compression Pass

Once the content is correct, try removing roughly 10-20% of the words
from prose that feels padded.

This is not a mandatory word-count target. Do not cut useful explanation
simply to meet it.

Look first for:

-   throat-clearing;
-   duplicated conclusions;
-   redundant modifiers;
-   obvious meta-commentary;
-   repeated context;
-   unnecessary transitions;
-   generic closing paragraphs.

The final version should feel denser in meaning, not merely shorter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 25. Do Not Overcorrect

Anti-AI rules can themselves make writing unnatural.

Do not mechanically ban:

-   every em dash;
-   every three-item list;
-   every contrast;
-   every transition;
-   every common phrase;
-   every polished sentence.

Humans use all of these.

The problem is repetition, predictability, emptiness, and using
rhetorical patterns in place of thought.

If a supposedly forbidden construction is clearly the best sentence, use
it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 26. Final Standard

Before returning substantial writing, ask:

> Would I keep every sentence if nobody were rewarding me for length,
> polish, or rhetorical flourish?

If not, cut or rewrite it.

The finished writing should feel like someone knew:

-   what they wanted to say;
-   why they were saying it;
-   who they were saying it to;
-   which details mattered;
-   and when to stop.
