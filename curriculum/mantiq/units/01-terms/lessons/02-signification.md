# Signification

A word denotes a meaning. The textbook calls this relation **signification** —
دَلَالَة (*dalālah*).

- The word that denotes is the **signifier** — دَال (*dāll*).
- The meaning denoted is the **signified** — مَدْلُوْل (*madlūl*).
- The word’s denoting that meaning is **signification** — دَلَالَة.

The same word may signify in three different ways. Context determines which
one is being used.

## 1. Complete signification

**دَلَالَة بِالْمُطَابَقَةِ** (*dalālah bi-l-muṭābaqah*)

The word denotes its meaning **in its entirety**.

### Worked example

> “She has memorised the Quran,” where she has memorised the entire Quran.

“Quran” denotes the whole of its meaning, so its signification is complete.

## 2. Partial signification

**دَلَالَة بِالتَّضَمُّنِ** (*dalālah bi-l-taḍammun*)

The word denotes **part of its meaning**.

### Worked example

> “She has memorised the Quran,” where she has memorised only part of it.

The sentence is unchanged, but the intended scope is part rather than the
whole. The signification is partial.

## 3. Concomitant signification

**دَلَالَة بِالِالْتِزَامِ** (*dalālah bi-l-iltizām*)

The word denotes something **customarily or logically associated** with its
direct meaning.

### Worked example

> “He is not human,” meaning that he has no feelings.

The speaker does not deny that the person belongs to the human species.
“Human” points to an associated quality, so the signification is concomitant.

## Discussion note

Concomitant usage is common in ordinary speech but can cause confusion in a
careful discussion. A single expression can also be used with different kinds
of signification by different speakers. When disagreement persists, ask what
part or associated meaning each person intends.

## Decision guide

Ask what the word denotes **in this context**:

1. The whole meaning → complete.
2. Part of the meaning → partial.
3. Something associated with the meaning → concomitant.
