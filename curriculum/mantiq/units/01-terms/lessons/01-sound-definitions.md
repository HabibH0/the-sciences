# Sound Definitions

A definition serves its purpose only when it draws the right boundary around a
term. It must include every thing that truly belongs to the term and keep out
every thing that does not belong. It must also explain the term without simply
repeating it.

## 1. Inclusive — جَامِع (*jāmiʿ*)

An inclusive definition covers **every valid member** of the category. If a
valid member is left outside, the definition is too restrictive.

### Worked example

> Proposed definition: A Muslim is someone who performs Hajj.

Some Muslims are unable to perform Hajj. They are valid members of the category
but the definition leaves them out. The definition is therefore **not
inclusive**.

## 2. Exclusive — مَانِع (*māniʿ*)

An exclusive definition keeps out **every invalid member**. If something that
does not belong still fits the words, the definition is too broad.

### Worked example

> Proposed definition: A Muslim is someone who believes in God.

This wording also fits Jews and Christians. It therefore includes people who
do not belong to the category being defined and is **not exclusive**.

## 3. Non-circular

A circular definition uses the very term being defined, or a form of it, as
part of the explanation. It sends the learner back to the starting point.

### Worked example

> Proposed definition: Weight is the amount that something weighs.

“Weighs” repeats the idea that still needs explanation. The definition is
**circular**.

## Why technical definitions matter

People often learn everyday terms by use rather than formal definition. In a
technical science, however, precise boundaries make reliable classification
possible. The textbook illustrates this with the boundary between a hill and a
mountain, and with the parts of the face that must be washed in *wuḍūʾ*.

## Guided check

For each proposed definition, ask in order:

1. Does it include every valid member?
2. Does it exclude every invalid member?
3. Does it avoid using the term being defined?

When all three answers are yes, the definition has passed the tests introduced
in this lesson.
