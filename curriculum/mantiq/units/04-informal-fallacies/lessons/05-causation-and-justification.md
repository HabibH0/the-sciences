# Causation, Chains, and Justification

**False cause** infers causation merely from one event following another. **Correlation-causation** infers causation from events varying together. A **slippery slope** reaches a dramatic endpoint through weak conditional links. **Two wrongs** defends conduct by pointing to another person's similar wrongdoing.

## Worked examples

An increase in ice-cream sales alongside shark attacks does not show that ice cream causes attacks; warm weather could affect both. A homework-to-homelessness chain requires evidence for every link. Another driver speeding does not justify one's own speeding.

## Guided check

List alternative causes, possible confounders, and the support for every causal link. Then test the proposed justification independently of what others did.

## Takeaway

Sequence, correlation, prediction, and excuse are not causal proof.
