# Formal and Informal Fallacies

A **formal fallacy** fails because of an argument's structure. An **informal fallacy** fails because of its content or method: an ambiguity, distraction, unsupported causal claim, irrelevant appeal, or another defect that cannot be detected from symbols alone.

## Worked comparison

Affirming the consequent is formal because every argument with that pattern is invalid. Attacking a speaker instead of their reasoning is informal because the defect depends on what the response says and whether it is relevant.

## Guided check

Ask whether the error survives when all topic words are replaced by S, P, and Q. If yes, it is likely formal; if the wording and context must be retained, it is likely informal.

## Takeaway

Classify the level of the error before naming the fallacy.
