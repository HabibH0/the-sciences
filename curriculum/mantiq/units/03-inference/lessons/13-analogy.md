# Analogy

An analogy compares a **source** case with a **target** case and transfers a conclusion because of shared features. Its strength depends on how many similarities and dissimilarities matter to the proposed conclusion.

## Worked example

Comparing alcohol with cocaine by intoxication uses a feature relevant to the ruling under discussion. Comparing cocaine with sugar because both can be white powders relies on superficial features and commits an analogical fallacy.

## Guided check

List source, target, conclusion, similarities, and dissimilarities. For each feature ask: would changing it affect the conclusion?

## Takeaway

Relevance matters more than surface resemblance.
