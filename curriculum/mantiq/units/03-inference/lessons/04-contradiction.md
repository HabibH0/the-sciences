# Contradiction and Its Conditions

The square of opposition compares A, E, I, and O propositions with the same subject and predicate. Contradictory pairs are A–O and E–I: exactly one member is true.

This truth rule applies only when the propositions match in subject, predicate, place, time, potentiality or actuality, condition, whole or part, and relationship. Context may require further unity, such as the same tool.

## Worked example

“Ahmad is walking now” and “Ahmad was not walking yesterday” are not contradictories because their times differ.

## Guided check

Before applying the square, compare every contextual aspect. Which single mismatch prevents contradiction?

## Takeaway

Opposite wording is insufficient; genuine contradiction requires unity in every relevant respect.
