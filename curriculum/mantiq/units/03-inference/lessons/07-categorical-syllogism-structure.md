# Categorical Syllogism Structure

A categorical syllogism has two premises, one conclusion, and three terms. The conclusion's subject is the **minor term** (S), its predicate the **major term** (P), and the term repeated in both premises is the **middle term** (M). The minor premise contains S; the major premise contains P.

## Worked example

From “All practising Muslims are honest” and “Ahmad is a practising Muslim,” conclude “Ahmad is honest.” Ahmad is S, honest is P, and practising Muslim is M.

An **enthymeme** leaves one of the three propositions unstated. Reconstruct it before testing the argument.

## Guided check

Begin with the conclusion to find S and P; only then locate M and name the premises.

## Takeaway

S and P come from the conclusion; M links the premises and disappears.
