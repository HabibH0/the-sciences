# Induction

**Complete induction** examines every member of a finite group and can yield a certain result. **Incomplete induction** samples only some members, so its conclusion remains probable and may be overturned by new data. Larger, relevant samples strengthen it.

One case used for a whole group is an **anecdotal fallacy**; too few cases produce **overgeneralisation**. The black-swan example shows how new observations can defeat an earlier generalisation.

**Abduction** differs by seeking the best probable explanation of existing data.

## Guided check

Identify the population, observed sample, claimed scope, and degree of certainty.

## Takeaway

Match confidence to coverage: all members can support certainty; a sample supports probability.
