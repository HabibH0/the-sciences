# Disjunctive Syllogisms

A disjunctive syllogism offers alternatives, then selects or rejects one. Valid moves depend on their relationship.

| Disjunction | Both possible? | Neither possible? | Affirming | Denying |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| strong | no | no | valid | valid |
| inclusive | yes | no | invalid | valid |
| exclusive non-exhaustive | no | yes | valid | invalid |

## Worked example

From “passport or licence” and “not passport,” licence follows if at least one is required. But possessing a passport does not exclude also having a licence.

Treating a non-exhaustive choice as exhaustive commits a **false dilemma**.

## Guided check

Ask two questions first: can both alternatives hold, and can neither hold?

## Takeaway

The word “or” does not determine the relationship by itself.
