# Hypothetical Syllogisms

In “If P, then Q,” P is the **antecedent** and Q the **consequent**. A mixed hypothetical syllogism combines this with a simple second premise.

| Second premise | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| P | Q | valid: modus ponens |
| not Q | not P | valid: modus tollens |
| Q | P | invalid: affirming the consequent |
| not P | not Q | invalid: denying the antecedent |

A pure hypothetical syllogism chains “If P then Q” with “If Q then R” to infer “If P then R.” The inference requires the intended connections to be strong enough; a merely usual connection does not guarantee the result.

## Guided check

Name what the second premise affirms or denies before deciding validity.

## Takeaway

Affirm P or deny Q; the two reversed moves are fallacies.
