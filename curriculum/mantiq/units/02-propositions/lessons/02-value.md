# Value — Truth and Falsity

The **value** of a proposition is either true or false. This concerns whether its judgement agrees with reality, not whether its grammatical form is well constructed.

## Worked example

“All squares have four sides” has a clear categorical structure and is true. “All squares have three sides” has the same structure but is false. Logic can analyse both; establishing a real-world premise may require evidence from the relevant field.

## Guided check

Compare “Some animals are mammals” with “Some triangles are circles.” Do not confuse identifying their form with deciding their value.

## Takeaway

Structure is internal to the proposition; truth or falsity depends on what it claims about reality.
