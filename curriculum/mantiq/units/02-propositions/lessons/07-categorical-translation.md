# Translation into Categorical Form

Ordinary sentences must often be normalised before analysis. Make both terms noun phrases, supply a clear quantifier, and use an explicit copula. An adjective can become “... things,” a verb “... people/things,” and an adverb a noun phrase describing its manner.

## Worked example

“Students work hard” can become “All students are hardworking people” when a universal reading is intended. If context does not fix “all” or “some,” it has a missing quantifier and should not be silently resolved.

“Only scholars are admitted” is scope-sensitive. It can mean that all admitted people are scholars; a different placement of “only” can produce the converse. Preserve both readings until context decides.

## Guided check

Translate “Some flowers smell sweetly.” Identify the nominalised expression and all four proposition components.

## Takeaway

Normalise grammar without adding meaning. Mark ambiguity instead of guessing.
