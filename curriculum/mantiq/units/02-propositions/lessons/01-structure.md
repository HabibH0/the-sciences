# Structure of a Proposition

A **proposition** (*qaḍiyyah*) expresses a judgement. In standard categorical form it has four working parts: a **quantifier**, **subject**, **copula**, and **predicate**.

A judgement makes a claim that can be true or false. A false claim still counts as a proposition. A question, an instruction, or a phrase that merely names something does not itself make a claim.

## Worked example

> All students are hardworking people.

“All” is the quantifier, “students” the subject, “are” the copula, and “hardworking people” the predicate. The proposition judges that the predicate applies to the subject class.

## Guided check

In “Some books are useful things,” label the four parts. Which word fixes scope? What class is discussed? What is said of it? What connects the two terms?

## Takeaway

First expose the four-part structure. Later properties depend on identifying these parts correctly.
