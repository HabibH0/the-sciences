# Lesson 23: أفعال الظن

## أفعال الرجحان

The second kind of `أفعال القلوب` are those that indicate ظن: assumption, likelihood, or a judgement the speaker considers probable. They are also called:

> **Core Text**
>
> الثَّانِي مَا تَدُلُّ عَلَى الظَّنِّ، وَيُسَمَّى أَفْعَالَ الرُّجْحَانِ

رجحان comes from the image of a scale tipping to one side. The speaker's mind inclines toward the judgement without settling into certainty.

## The verbs

| Verb | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| ظَنَّ | ﴿الَّذِينَ يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُوا رَبِّهِمْ﴾ | those who believe they will meet their Lord |
| حَسِبَ | ﴿وَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُهْتَدُونَ﴾ | and they think they are guided |
| زَعَمَ | ﴿زَعَمَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنْ لَنْ يُبْعَثُوا﴾ | those who disbelieved claimed they would never be raised |
| جَعَلَ | ﴿وَجَعَلُوا الْمَلَائِكَةَ إِنَاثًا﴾ | and they made the angels female |
| حَجَا، عَدَّ، هَبْ، خَالَ | used in classical Arabic for supposing or considering | — |

Each takes two objects originally a `مبتدأ` and `خبر`.

## ظن in the Quran used for certainty

The word ظن normally indicates assumption. Yet ﴿الَّذِينَ يَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُلَاقُوا رَبِّهِمْ﴾ is praising the believers, and belief in the resurrection must be held with complete certainty. Someone who merely thinks resurrection probable has `شك`, not `إيمان`.

To understand this, two types of knowledge are distinguished:

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| علم البديهي | immediate, self-evident knowledge; no reasoning required |
| علم النظري | certainty reached through reasoning, evidence, and inference |

One plus one is two: that is علم بديهي. Working out that the square root of 144 is 12 is علم نظري. The conclusion is fully certain, but it was reached through reasoning rather than direct perception.

Belief in the resurrection is علم نظري. The believer works through the evidence: there must be a creator, the creator sent a messenger, the messenger conveyed a message, and that message is true. From this he arrives at full certainty. Because the certainty was reached via `استدلال` rather than immediate perception, the word ظن is used to express it in those Quranic contexts. This is also why faith in these matters is `إيمان بالغيب`. If a person were seeing the angels, the reckoning and the gathering directly, that would be `رؤية` and `علم`, not `إيمان`.

Elsewhere the same content is expressed with `علم` instead, where Allah commands the believers to know it with certainty because He is the one informing them.

> **Careful Box**
>
> This is a rule of thumb, not a universal rule. Not every use of ظن in the Quran means certainty. The context determines whether the word carries its ordinary meaning of assumption or the stronger meaning of علم نظري.

## The scale of تأكيد: أَنَّ, أَنْ, and two objects

When `عَلِمَ` or `ظَنَّ` is followed by a clause, the form of the connecting particle affects the level of تأكيد:

> **Rule Box**
>
> زيادةُ اللفظِ تدلُّ على زيادةِ المعنى
>
> More wording indicates more intensity of meaning.

`أَنَّ` carries a شدّة (doubled letter). `أَنْ` is the mukhaffafa form without it. Dropping both and using two plain objects is lighter still.

### With عَلِمَ

| Structure | Level of تأكيد | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| عَلِمَ + أَنَّ | strongest | ﴿فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ﴾ | so know that there is no god but Allah |
| عَلِمَ + أَنْ | lighter | ﴿عَلِمَ أَنْ سَيَكُونُ مِنْكُمْ مَرْضَى﴾ | He knew that some of you would be ill |
| عَلِمَ + مفعولين | lightest | ﴿فَإِنْ عَلِمْتُمُوهُنَّ مُؤْمِنَاتٍ﴾ | if you know them to be believing women |

In ﴿فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ﴾ the taqeed is strong. This is a command to know with the heaviest certainty, because the statement is the foundation of faith.

In ﴿عَلِمَ أَنْ سَيَكُونُ مِنْكُمْ مَرْضَى﴾ the passage is about Allah granting ease to the believers after the difficulty of the night prayer. The point is gentleness and accommodation, not emphasis. Placing heavy taqeed on the fact of illness would work against that tone, so the lighter `أَنْ` fits the purpose.

﴿فَإِنْ عَلِمْتُمُوهُنَّ مُؤْمِنَاتٍ﴾ concerns the treaty of Hudaybiyah. Under its terms, whoever came from Mecca to Medina was to be sent back, while whoever left Medina for Mecca would not be returned. Women were never named in the treaty, so when believing women came, the Muslims were told not to return them. Here `عَلِمَ` takes two plain objects with no particle at all, and the lightest structure suits the ruling: the believers were not asked to open up those women's hearts, only to establish by ordinary questioning that they were believers.

> **Careful Box**
>
> The choice between `أَنَّ`, `أَنْ` and two objects is not about the speaker's level of certainty. Allah knows all things with certainty in every case. The choice reflects the context and what the statement is meant to achieve.

### With ظَنَّ

The same scale applies to ظن.

| Structure | Level of تأكيد | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ظَنَّ + أَنَّ | strongest | ﴿وَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ مَانِعَتُهُمْ حُصُونُهُمْ﴾ | and they thought their fortresses would protect them |
| ظَنَّ + أَنْ | lighter | ﴿مَا ظَنَنْتُمْ أَنْ يَخْرُجُوا﴾ | you did not think they would leave |
| ظَنَّ + مفعولين | lightest | ﴿وَمَا أَظُنُّ السَّاعَةَ قَائِمَةً﴾ | and I do not think the Hour will be established |

The first two come from the same passage, about `بنو النضير`, a Jewish tribe of Medina. They held fortresses, were firmly established, and had a grip on the finances of the city. After they broke their treaties they were expelled.

Addressing the Ansar, Allah says ﴿مَا ظَنَنْتُمْ أَنْ يَخْرُجُوا﴾: you did not think they would leave. The lighter `أَنْ` matches the meaning, because the Ansar did not entertain even a slight thought of it.

Of `بنو النضير` themselves Allah says ﴿وَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُمْ مَانِعَتُهُمْ حُصُونُهُمْ﴾. Here `أَنَّ` is used, and the wording is strengthened further by putting `أَنَّهُمْ` first, so that `مَانِعَة` follows as an `اسم فاعل` operating on `حُصُونُهُمْ`. The ordinary order would be `أَنَّ حُصُونَهُمْ مَانِعَتُهُمْ`. Their conviction was much firmer, and the wording carries that.

﴿وَمَا أَظُنُّ السَّاعَةَ قَائِمَةً﴾ has neither particle. It carries the least تأكيد of the three, which suits a speaker simply denying the Hour rather than pressing a settled conviction.

> **Careful Box**
>
> This remains a rule of thumb. A speaker may depart from it in a particular place in order to bring out one point over another.

## حَسِبَ

The root of `حسب` is `حساب`: to calculate, count, or reckon, as in `حساب يسير`, an easy reckoning.

`حسب` as a `فعل قلب` is built on that sense. It points to a judgement arrived at by weighing up what is observed, rather than a bare thought held in the mind.

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿وَيَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُمْ مُهْتَدُونَ﴾
>
> And they reckon themselves to be guided.

They look at their books, their scripture and their own deeds, and from all of it they calculate that they are on guidance.

The exact line between ظن and حسب is hard to draw. As a general observation, ظن is more purely mental, while حسب attaches to `علامات` and signs that are weighed up.

## زَعَمَ

`زعم` is also a `فعل قلب`. In the Quran, what is claimed under زعم is regularly something `باطل`: the verb carries the sense of thinking something and being wrong about it.

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿زَعَمَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنْ لَنْ يُبْعَثُوا﴾
>
> Those who disbelieved claimed that they would never be resurrected.

`زعم` regularly comes with `أن` or `أنّ` rather than appearing with two separate `مفعول به` elements.

## جَعَلَ

`جعل` may be used as a `فعل قلب` meaning to assume or consider something to be something.

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿وَجَعَلُوا الْمَلَائِكَةَ الَّذِينَ هُمْ عِبَادُ الرَّحْمَنِ إِنَاثًا﴾
>
> And they made the angels, who are the servants of the Most Merciful, female.

They did not create or manufacture the angels. They assumed them to be female. The same applies when someone is said to have made a thing his god: he did not build it, he took it and considered it to be his god.

Most of the time `جعل` carries the meaning of `تحويل`, changing one thing into another state, and it may also mean to create. Which reading applies depends on the meaning intended in that place.

## حَجَا, عَدَّ, هَبْ, خَالَ

These are also counted among `أفعال القلوب`, though they are not common in the Quran.

| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| هَبْ | suppose, imagine, as in هَبْ أَنَّكَ |
| خَالَ | to think |
| عَدَّ | to count or reckon |
| حَجَا | listed in the books among these verbs, though rarely met in practice |
