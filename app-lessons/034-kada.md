# Lesson 34: أفعال المقاربة — كاد

أفعال المقاربة are the third group of الأفعال الناقصة, following on from أفعال الرجاء. There are three of them: كاد, كرب, and أوشك.

> **Definition Box**
>
> **الضَّرْبُ الثَّالِثُ أَفْعَالُ الْمُقَارَبَةِ، وَهِيَ مَا وُضِعَتْ لِلدَّلَالَةِ عَلَى قُرْبِ وُقُوعِ الْخَبَرِ**  
> These are verbs coined to show that the action named by the خبر is close to occurring.

## الأول: كاد

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿وَكَادُوا يَقْتُلُونَنِي﴾
>
> They were about to kill me.

These are the words of هارون عليه السلام to his brother موسى.

## كاد and عسى: shared مقاربة, different paths

> **Rule Box**
>
> **عَسَى عَلَى سَبِيلِ الرَّجَاءِ، وَكَادَ وَأَخَوَاتُهَا عَلَى سَبِيلِ الْحُصُولِ**

Both عسى and كاد carry مَعنى الْمُقَارَبَة, nearness, and both point toward the future. They differ in what kind of nearness each expresses.

عسى moves عَلَى سَبِيلِ الرَّجَاء: it states a hope, something the متكلم wishes for or fears, and whether it comes about or not is left open. كاد moves عَلَى سَبِيلِ الْحُصُول: it states, as a fact, how close something actually came to happening, regardless of whether it then happened or not. عسى speaks in the subjunctive; كاد speaks factually.

| Verb | Meanings carried |
|---|---|
| كاد | الْمُقَارَبَة only |
| عَسَى | الْمُقَارَبَة, together with التَّرَجِّي, الْإِشْفَاق, and التَّعْلِيل |
| لَعَلَّ | التَّرَجِّي, الْإِشْفَاق, and التَّعْلِيل, without الْمُقَارَبَة |

## كاد المثبت وكاد المنفي

> **Rule Box**
>
> **الْمُثْبَتُ عَلَى نَفْيِ وُقُوعِ الْفِعْلِ مَعَ مُقَارَبَةِ الْوُقُوعِ**  
> When كاد is مُثْبَت, affirmative and not negated, it shows that the فعل did not happen, but that it came close to happening.

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿وَكَادُوا يَقْتُلُونَنِي﴾
>
> They nearly killed me.

هارون is saying that his people did not actually kill him, but they came close to it.

> **Rule Box**
>
> **وَالْمَنْفِيُّ عَلَى نَفْيِ وُقُوعِ الْفِعْلِ مَعَ نَفْيِ مُقَارَبَةِ الْوُقُوعِ، فَهُوَ آكَدُ فِي النَّفْيِ**  
> When كاد is مَنْفِيّ, negated with لا يكاد or لم يكد, the negation goes further: not only did the فعل not happen, it did not even come close to happening. This is a stronger form of negation than denying the verb on its own.

> **Example Box**
>
> لَمْ يَكَدْ يَرَاهَا — he could hardly see it.
>
> From ﴿ظُلُمَاتٌ بَعْضُهَا فَوْقَ بَعْضٍ إِذَا أَخْرَجَ يَدَهُ لَمْ يَكَدْ يَرَاهَا﴾, describing the darkness of كفر: someone extending his hand cannot even come close to seeing it. The darkness is total.

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿وَيُسْقَى مِن مَّاءٍ صَدِيدٍ يَتَجَرَّعُهُ وَلَا يَكَادُ يُسِيغُهُ﴾
>
> He will be given a drink of pus, gulping it down, hardly able to swallow it.

The people of جهنم are given مَاءٍ صَدِيد, water mixed with pus, to drink. وَلَا يَكَادُ يُسِيغُهُ means that at first he does not come anywhere close to swallowing it, because it is so repulsive. The word that follows it, يَتَجَرَّعُهُ, shows that he does gulp it down eventually, driven by the severity of his thirst. Although the verse denies that he comes close to swallowing it, the surrounding context makes clear that he ends up swallowing it regardless.

> **Careful Box**
>
> The نفي only describes how the matter began. What ultimately happens is understood separately, from the rest of the sentence or from context, not from the negated verb itself.

## Understanding the negation from context

> **Rule Box**
>
> **قَدْ يُفْهَمُ وُقُوعُ الْفِعْلِ بَعْدَ نَفْيِ مُقَارَبَةِ الْوُقُوعِ**  
> An action can still be understood to have happened, even after كاد has denied that it came close to happening.

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿فَذَبَحُوهَا وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ﴾
>
> So they slaughtered it, though they nearly had not done it.

This is from the story in سورة البقرة of بني إسرائيل and the cow. They kept raising objection after objection instead of simply slaughtering it, so Allah describes them with وَمَا كَادُوا يَفْعَلُونَ: at first, they were nowhere near doing it. Even so, the verse that follows, فَذَبَحُوهَا, makes clear that they slaughtered it in the end. The negation of nearness describes how the matter began, not how it finished.
