# Lesson 131: Al-Maf'ul Lahu (المفعول له)

المفعول له ("the object of reason" — literally, the object *for whose sake* the verb happened) is the fourth of the منصوبات, coming right after المفعول فيه. Where المفعول فيه answers *when* or *where*, المفعول له answers a different question about the verb entirely — *why* — and it goes by a second name, **مفعول لأجله**, used interchangeably with the first throughout the books of تفسير and إعراب.

> **Definition Box**
>
> المفعول له: هو ما وقع لأجله الفعل — that which the verb's action was performed *for*: the reason or motive (السبب أو الباعث) that lay behind it, نحو: ﴿وَالَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا ابْتِغَاءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِمْ﴾ — "And those who are patient, seeking the countenance of their Lord." Here "ابتغاء" ("seeking") is the reason صَبَروا happened — the motive behind the patience, not the patience itself.
>
> ويُسمَّى المفعول له أيضًا **مفعولًا لأجله** — the two names are used interchangeably in books of تفسير and إعراب.

## شرائط نصب المفعول له بلا واسطة اللام

Not every noun that expresses a reason automatically takes نصب. Direct نصب — without a لام or another حرف جر standing in the way — has specific conditions attached to it; if even one of those conditions breaks down, the noun still carries the *meaning* of المفعول له, but it is no longer نُصِب — instead it is جُرَّ باللام أو نحوها.

> **Rule Box**
>
> يُشترط لنصب المفعول له بلا واسطة أن يكون:
>
> **١) مصدرًا.** If it isn't a مصدر, it cannot take نصب — بخلاف: ﴿وَالْأَرْضَ وَضَعَهَا لِلْأَنَامِ﴾ — "And the earth He laid out for the creatures." "الأنام" is اسم ذات (a noun naming created beings), not a مصدر — so it had to be جُرّ باللام, and could not be نُصِب.
>
> **٢) مصدرًا قلبيًّا** (i.e. from أفعال القلب — verbs of the heart, such as will, desire, fear — rather than أفعال الجوارح, verbs of the physical limbs). بخلاف: سافرتُ إلى مكة للحجِّ — "I traveled to Makkah for الحج." "الحج" is indeed a مصدر, but it names a فعل جارحة (a bodily act), not a فعل قلب — so even though it's connected to the meaning of المفعول له, it does not take نصب.
>
> **٣) أن يتحد فاعل الفعل وفاعل المصدر** — the doer of the main verb and the doer of the مصدر must be one and the same. بخلاف: ﴿وَالْأَنْعَامَ خَلَقَهَا لَكُمْ ۖ ... وَالْخَيْلَ وَالْبِغَالَ وَالْحَمِيرَ لِتَرْكَبُوهَا وَزِينَةً﴾ — "And the grazing livestock He created for you... and horses, mules, and donkeys for you to ride and as adornment." "لكم" isn't even a مصدر to begin with, so اللام was required regardless.
>
> **٤) أن يتحد زمن الفعل وزمن المصدر** — the time of the main verb and the time of the مصدر must match. بخلاف: أرحمُ والدي كبيرًا لرحمتِه إيّايَ صغيرًا — "I show mercy to my father in his old age, because of his mercy toward me when I was young." Here the فاعل of "أرحم" (I) and the فاعل of "رحمته" (he) are different persons, and the زمن of الفعل (present — أرحم) doesn't match the زمن of المصدر (past — رحمته, back when I was young). With both اتحاد الفاعل and اتحاد الزمن broken at once, "رحمته" cannot be نُصِب as مفعول له.

> **Careful Box**
>
> On ﴿لِتَرْكَبُوهَا وَزِينَةً﴾, أهل العلم record two ways of إعراب-ing "زينة": either it is مفعول له, معطوفة on the محل of "لتركبوها" (أي: خلقها لتركبوها ولزينتكم — "He created them for you to ride, and for your adornment"), or it is مفعول مطلق for a dropped verb, تقديره: وتتزيَّنوا بها زينةً ("and you adorn yourselves with them, an adornment"). This is one example of how a single ayah can rightly bear more than one correct إعراب.

## تقدير المضاف المحذوف: مفعول له بمصدر مؤول

A مصدر مؤول can occur after "أنْ" and be إعراب-ed as مفعول له, on the assumption of a dropped مضاف standing before it — usually a word like "كراهة" ("aversion to").

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿وَأَلْقَىٰ فِي الْأَرْضِ رَوَاسِيَ أَن تَمِيدَ بِكُمْ﴾ — "And He cast into the earth firmly set mountains, lest it shift with you." أي: كراهةَ أن تميدَ بكم — "out of aversion to it shifting with you": Allah set the firmly-anchored mountains into the earth so that it would not shake with its people.

> **Careful Box**
>
> The supplied word here ("كراهة") is not wording the ayah itself contains — it is a conclusion أهل اللغة drew from context and from the words of السلف in تفسير, not free reasoning about the meaning. This is an example that shows the importance of returning to تفسير السلف and understanding the Arabs' own style of speech before interpreting passages like this one, rather than assuming a meaning from oneself.
