# Lesson 4: Signs of the اسم

## What is an اسم?

Define `اسم` like this:

> **Definition Box**
>
> الاسمُ مَا دَلَّ عَلَى مَعْنًى فِي نَفْسِهِ غَيْرِ مُقْتَرِنٍ بِزَمَانٍ

An `اسم` is a word that has its own meaning and is not tied to tense.

This separates `اسم` from `فعل`. A `فعل` also carries meaning in itself, but its form is connected to `زمان`. An `اسم` carries meaning without its form telling you `ماضي`, `حال`, or `استقبال`.

> **Example Box**
>
> `الله`, `الرَّحْمٰن`, and `الرَّحِيم` are أسماء.  
> They carry meaning, but their forms do not indicate `ماضي`, `حال`, or `استقبال`.

## The main signs

An `اسم` can be recognised through several signs:

| Sign | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| الجر | the word can be مجرور | بِسْمِ اللهِ | in the name of Allah |
| أل | it accepts أل التعريف | الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ | the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful |
| التنوين | it accepts normal tanwin | هُدًى | guidance |
| الإسناد إليه | something can be said about it | إِنَّ إِبْرَاهِيمَ لَحَلِيمٌ | indeed Ibrahim is forbearing |
| النداء | it can be called | يَا نُوحُ | O Nuh |
| الإضافة | it can be مضاف | رَبِّ العَالَمِينَ | Lord of the worlds |

One valid sign is enough. A word does not need to show every sign at once.

These signs are practical tools. If a word accepts one of them correctly, that is enough to recognise it as an `اسم`.

> **Careful Box**
>
> The signs are signs of the word itself.  
> For example, `الجر` means the word is actually مجرور, not merely that a جار appears somewhere nearby.

`الإسناد إليه` means the word can be the one something is said about, so it can stand as `مبتدأ` or as `فاعل`. In `إن إبراهيم لحليم`, the word `إبراهيم` is the one being spoken about, so it is `مسند إليه`.

## Notes on أل

The usual marker of definition in Arabic is `أل`, as in:

> **Example Box**
>
> `الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ`

There are two useful side points:

| Point | Explanation |
|---|---|
| `أم` في لغة حمير | Some people of Himyar in Yemen used `أم` as حرف تعريف instead of `أل`. |
| What exactly is the marker? | Grammarians differed: Sībawayh treated the لام as the marker, with the hamzah added only because a word cannot begin with a silent letter; al-Khalīl treated الألف واللام together as the marker; al-Mubarrad treated the open hamzah as the marker, with the lām added to distinguish it from همزة الاستفهام. |

The Himyari usage is preserved in a narration of the Prophet ﷺ:

> **Example Box**
>
> «لَيْسَ مِنَ امْبِرِّ امْصِيَامُ فِي امْسَفَرِ»
>
> Here `امبر` stands for `البر`, `امصيام` for `الصيام`, and `امسفر` for `السفر`.

For practical reading, `أل` is the normal sign to recognise.

## Note on tanwin

The `تنوين` listed as علامة الاسم is the normal grammatical `تنوين`.

> **Careful Box**
>
> `تنوين الترنم` is different. It appears at the ends of poetic lines for sound and rhythm, so it is not the `تنوين` meant here as علامة الاسم.

Poetry allows what ordinary speech does not:

> **Rule Box**
>
> يُغْتَفَرُ فِي الشِّعْرِ مَا لا يُغْتَفَرُ فِي غَيْرِهِ

> **Example Box**
>
> أَقِلِّي اللَّوْمَ عَاذِلَ وَالعِتَابَا  
> وَقُولِي إِنْ أَصَبْتُ لَقَدْ أَصَابَا
>
> "Lessen your blame, O critic, and your reproach; and say, if I have hit the mark, he has indeed hit the mark."

`أقلي` is فعل أمر meaning "lessen", used here in the sense of "stop". `عاذل` is منادى. `العتاب` is معطوف on `اللوم`. The added ن sound at the end of `والعتابا` and `لقد أصابا` is `تنوين الترنم`, put there so the line matches the rhyme. It is not showing نكرة: `العتاب` already carries `أل`, and `أصاب` is a فعل. Since this تنوين can attach even to a فعل or a حرف, it is not the تنوين meant as علامة الاسم.

## Visual tarkeeb: بِسْمِ اللهِ

| بِـ | اسْمِ | اللهِ |
|---|---|---|
| حرف جر | اسم مجرور بالباء | مضاف إليه مجرور |

`اسم` is مجرور because of `بـ`, showing الجر. `لَفْظُ الجَلالَةِ اللهِ` is مجرور because it is مضاف إليه.

> **Careful Box**
>
> The sign is not merely that a حرف جر appears nearby.  
> The sign is that the word itself is مجرور.

## Visual tarkeeb: رَبِّ العَالَمِينَ

| رَبِّ | العَالَمِينَ |
|---|---|
| مضاف | مضاف إليه |

Being `مضاف` is a sign that `رب` is an اسم.

In an إضافة, the first word is the `مضاف` and the second is the `مضاف إليه`. Since `رب` can stand as the first part of an إضافة, it shows اسم behaviour.

## Two important cautions

### 1. الفعل والحرف do not become مجرور

The rule is:

> **Rule Box**
>
> الفِعْلُ وَالحَرْفُ لا يَكُونَانِ مَجْرُورَيْنِ، وَإِنْ دَخَلَ عَلَيْهِمَا جَارٌّ

A `فعل` and a `حرف` do not become مجرور, even if a جار enters upon a structure containing them. What is مجرور in such cases is the `اسم مؤول`, not the `فعل` or the `حرف` on its own.

> **Example Box**
>
> `لِيَكُونَ لِلْعَالَمِينَ نَذِيرًا`
>
> The لام comes before `يكون`, but `يكون` is not مجرور. It is منصوب by an `أن مقدرة`, and the لام enters upon the whole `اسم مؤول`.

> **Example Box**
>
> `وَاشْهَدْ بِأَنَّا مُسْلِمُونَ`
>
> `أنّ` is حرف مشبه بالفعل, and the باء does not make `أنّ` itself مجرور.  
> The whole `مصدر مؤول` is treated as مجرور بالباء.

### 2. يا does not always mean the next word is منادى

For example:

> **Careful Box**
>
> قَدْ تَدْخُلُ أَحْرُفُ النِّدَاءِ عَلَى غَيْرِ المُنَادَى
>
> يَا لَيْتَ قَوْمِي يَعْلَمُونَ

In `يا ليت`, the word `ليت` is not being called. The `يا` here strengthens the wish and the grief behind it, the way a person naturally stretches the voice when distressed. So the sign is being truly `منادى`, not merely appearing after `يا`.

Grammarians explain the `يا` in this verse in two ways: it may be `حرف تنبيه`, or it may be `حرف نداء` with the `منادى` omitted, understood as something like `يا هؤلاء، ليتني`. On either explanation, `ليت` itself is not a منادى.
