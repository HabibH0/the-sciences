# Lesson 123: حذف عامل الحال

الحال ("the circumstantial") is normally governed by a verb or شبه الفعل — the action-word that the circumstance describes. But just as المفعول به and المفعول المطلق can lose their governing verb in certain fixed patterns, الحال too has its own set of situations where the عامل is dropped and only الحال itself remains on the surface. This is the third branch (الضرب الثالث) of حذف العامل المعنوي.

> **Rule Box**
>
> وَيُحْذَفُ عامِلُ الحالِ **قِياسًا وُجوبًا** في أَرْبَعِ مَواضِعَ — the governing element of الحال is dropped by regular rule (قياسًا) and obligatorily (وجوبًا) in **four** fixed situations. Beyond these four, one further pattern is dropped only سَماعًا (by attested usage, not by a general rule).

## الأول: أن تسد الحال مسد الخبر

> **Definition Box**
>
> When الحال itself **stands in for the خبر** of a nominal sentence — that is, the جملة has no visible خبر at all, and الحال supplies its meaning instead — the عامل governing that حال is obligatorily dropped.

> **Example Box**
>
> مِنْهُ قَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «أَقْرَبُ ما يَكونُ العَبْدُ مِنْ رَبِّهِ وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ» — "The servant is closest to his Lord while he is prostrating."
>
> **إعراب:** أَقْرَبُ is مبتدأ؛ ما is مصدرية؛ يَكونُ is a فعل ناقص whose اسم is العَبْدُ؛ مِنْ رَبِّهِ is متعلق بـ أَقْرَبَ. Now — where is الخبر of أَقْرَبُ؟ There isn't one written. Instead, وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ is حالٌ مِنَ العَبْدِ, and this حال **سادٌّ مَسَدَّ الخَبَرِ** — it fills in for, "stands in the place of," the missing خبر. The full sense is: أَيْ أَقْرَبُ حالِ العَبْدِ مِنْ رَبِّهِ **حاصِلٌ لَهُ** وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ — "the closest state of the servant to his Lord **is attained** [for him] while he is prostrating." حاصِلٌ is the dropped خبر itself, and it is that dropped حاصِلٌ — not أَقْرَبُ — that actually governs وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ as its حال.

> **Careful Box**
>
> It's tempting to read أَقْرَبُ ما يَكونُ العَبْدُ مِنْ رَبِّهِ **وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ** by simply translating وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ as if it were the خبر itself ("the closest a servant can be to his Lord *is* when he is prostrating") — and this is in fact how many students first render it. But that isn't the real إعراب: وَهُوَ ساجِدٌ is grammatically a حال (a جملة اسمية حالية), not a خبر. It only reads naturally *as if* it supplied the خبر's meaning because the true خبر — a dropped حاصِلٌ ("attained," "occurring") — has been removed, precisely because this حال was doing its job well enough that the sentence stayed clear without it. Keeping the two apart (what the sentence *means* versus what it is *grammatically doing*) is what this whole موضع is really testing.

## الثاني: أن تدل بلفظها على زيادة أو نقص بتدريج

> **Rule Box**
>
> When الحال's own wording expresses a **gradual increase or decrease** — literally, the word itself carries the sense of "and rising" or "and more" — its عامل is dropped.
>
> فائِدَةٌ: العامِلُ المَحْذوفُ هُوَ **يَزيدُ** أَوْ نَحْوُهُ — the dropped verb is (something like) يَزيدُ, "it increases." وَشَرْطُ هذِهِ الحالِ أَن تَكونَ مَصْحوبَةً **بِالْفاءِ أَوْ ثُمَّ** — and this حال must be introduced by فَ or (less commonly) ثُمَّ.

> **Example Box**
>
> تَصَدَّقْ بِدِرْهَمٍ فَصاعِدًا — "Give a dirham in charity, or more." أَيْ: تَصَدَّقْ فَرْدَ صاعِدًا (تصدق، ثم [الأمر] يزيد صاعدا) — the dropped عامل is understood as يَزيدُ ("it increases," "it climbs"), with صاعِدًا itself literally meaning "climbing, rising" — the very word carries the sense of gradual increase that triggers the drop.

## الثالث: أن تكون مسبوقة باستفهام يراد به التوبيخ

> **Rule Box**
>
> When الحال is preceded by an **استفهام (question) meant as تَوْبيخ** — a rhetorical, scolding question, not a genuine request for information — its عامل is dropped.
>
> فائِدَةٌ: العامِلُ المَحْذوفُ هُوَ **بَقِيتَ** أَوْ **لَبِثْتَ** أَوْ **لَمْ تَزَلْ** وَنَحْوُهُما — the dropped verb is something like بَقِيتَ/لَبِثْتَ ("you remained/stayed") or لَمْ تَزَلْ ("you have not ceased to be").

> **Example Box**
>
> أَقاعِدًا وَقَدْ أُقيمَتِ الصَّلاةُ؟ — "[Are you still] sitting, while the prayer has already started?!" أَيْ: أَلَمْ تَزَلْ قاعِدًا وَقَدْ أُقيمَتِ الصَّلاةُ؟ — this isn't really asking *whether* the person is sitting (that's already obvious) — it's a rebuke: why on earth are you still sitting?

## الرابع: أن تكون مؤكدة لمضمون الجملة

> **Rule Box**
>
> When الحال **reaffirms the very meaning already contained** in the جملة that precedes it — restating, in effect, what the sentence already says — its عامل is dropped.
>
> فائِدَةٌ: وَيُشْتَرَطُ فيهِ شَرْطانِ — two conditions apply here: **(1)** أَنْ يَتَّفِقَ مَعْنَى الحالِ وَمَعْنَى الجُمْلَةِ — the meaning of الحال must genuinely match the meaning of the جملة before it (not just relate to it loosely); and **(2)** أَنْ يَكونَ المُبْتَدَأُ وَالخَبَرُ **مَعْرِفَتَيْنِ جامِدَتَيْنِ** — the مبتدأ and خبر of that preceding جملة must both be معرفة (definite) and جامد (non-derived nouns, not مشتق/action-based words like اسم فاعل).

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿وَهُوَ الحَقُّ مُصَدِّقًا لِما مَعَهُمْ﴾ — "And it is the truth, confirming what is with them." الحَقُّ ("the truth") already implies truthfulness/confirmation; مُصَدِّقًا ("confirming") as حال simply reaffirms — doesn't add new information to — that same sense of truthfulness already packed into الحَقُّ.
>
> خالِدٌ أَبوكَ عَطوفًا — "Khālid is your father, [being] affectionate." خالِدٌ (معرفة، جامد) and أَبوكَ (معرفة، جامد) satisfy the second condition; and the very fact that خالِد **is your father** already implies tenderness and care — so عَطوفًا (affectionate) as حال doesn't add new information, it simply reaffirms what "being your father" already means. This exact example is the standard one repeated across النحو textbooks for this موضع precisely because it's hard to render smoothly in translation without first grasping the الاستعمال (usage) itself.

## سماعا: مواضع أخرى

> **Rule Box**
>
> وَقَدْ يُحْذَفُ العامِلُ **سَماعًا** في غَيْرِ ذلِكَ — beyond these four قياسي (rule-governed) مواضع, the عامل of الحال may also be dropped in other expressions, but only because they are attested that way (سماعًا) — not because they follow a general, extendable rule.

> **Example Box**
>
> هَنيئًا لَكَ — "[May it be] pleasant/wholesome for you" — a fixed congratulatory expression.
>
> سالِمًا غانِمًا — "[Returning] safe and victorious" — said of someone who has come back from a difficult journey or battle unharmed and successful; both سالما and غانما are حال with a dropped عامل (something like رَجَعَ, "he returned").
