# Lesson 64: كأنّ — حرف التشبيه

The third of the seven حروف تنصب الاسم وترفع الخبر is a familiar sight from ordinary speech: كَأَنَّ.

> **Definition Box**
>
> كَأَنَّ حَرْفٌ لِلتَّشْبِيهِ

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿الزُّجَاجَةُ كَأَنَّهَا كَوْكَبٌ دُرِّيٌّ﴾
>
> The glass, as if it were a pearly star.

Here الزُّجاجَةُ (the glass housing the lamp) is likened to كَوْكَبٌ دُرِّيٌّ, a brilliant star — كَأَنَّ is what carries that التشبيه.

## آكد في التشبيه من الكاف

> **Rule Box**
>
> كَأَنَّ آكَدُ فِي التَّشْبِيهِ مِنَ الكَافِ، لِأَنَّهُ مُرَكَّبَةٌ مِنَ الكَافِ وَأَنَّ عَلَى قَوْلٍ، وَقِيلَ بَسِيطَةٌ

Plain الكاف — the حرف جر studied earlier — can itself express تشبيه (as in زَيْدٌ كَالْأَسَدِ, "Zayd is like a lion"). كَأَنَّ says the same thing, but says it more emphatically, and the reason traces back to a familiar principle: زِيَادَةُ اللَّفْظِ تَدُلُّ عَلَى زِيَادَةِ المَعْنَى, "more in the wording signals more in the meaning" (also phrased زِيَادَةُ المَبْنَى تَدُلُّ عَلَى زِيَادَةِ المَعْنَى). الكاف alone is a single letter; كَأَنَّ is more.

> **Careful Box**
>
> Grammarians differ on why كَأَنَّ carries that extra force. One view holds it مُرَكَّبَةٌ — literally compounded from two elements, الكاف (comparison) plus أَنَّ (توكيد), fused into one word: ك + أَنَّ = كَأَنَّ, "surely, like." On this view the extra تأكيد is built directly into its makeup — it is doing the work of two particles at once. The other view holds it بَسِيطَةٌ, a single simple word in its own right that merely resembles two words without actually being composed of them. Either way, the شراح agree on the conclusion: كَأَنَّ carries more تأكيد in its تشبيه than الكاف does alone.
