# Lesson 26: أفعال التحويل

## What are أفعال التحويل?

أفعال التحويل are verbs that carry the meaning of transformation: changing one thing from one state into another. Like أفعال القلوب, they take two objects that were originally a مبتدأ and خبر, which is why they are often treated as a branch of أفعال القلوب rather than a separate class.

> **Core Text**
>
> أَفْعَالُ التَّحْوِيلِ: مَا تَكُونُ بِمَعْنَى صَيَّرَ، أَيْ حَوَّلَ الشَّيْءَ مِنْ حَالٍ إِلَى حَالٍ

The first object is the thing being transformed. The second object is the new state it is put into. The change may be an actual one or a change in judgement, role, or status.

## The verbs

### صيّر — to change, to convert

The base transformation verb, from `باب تفعيل`. It changes the first object into the condition named by the second object.

> **Core Text**
>
> صَيَّرَ عَذْبَ الشَّرَابِ مُرًّا وَزَادَ ضِيقَ الْحَيَاةِ ضِيقًا

| صَيَّرَ | عَذْبَ الشَّرَابِ | مُرًّا |
|---|---|---|
| فعل تحويل | مفعول به أول | مفعول به ثاني |

*It turned the sweetness of drink into bitterness, and it made the constraints of life tighter still.*

This line is a poetic `شاهد`. The `فاعل` of `صيّر` is a `هو` referring back to what the poem is describing.

### ردّ — to return to a previous state

ردّ means to change back: to return something to a state it was in before. A short نحو text may simply gloss ردّ as بمعنى صيّر, but two words are not used for exactly the same meaning. Each carries its own connotation, and here the connotation is that the second state is one the object already held.

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿وَدَّ كَثِيرٌ مِّنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ لَوْ يَرُدُّونَكُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ إِيمَانِكُمْ كُفَّارًا﴾
>
> Many of the People of the Book wished they could return you, after your belief, to disbelief.

Most of those addressed had been in disbelief before accepting Islam. ردّ here says: take them back to what they were. Had the verse said `لَوْ يُصَيِّرُونَكُمْ` or `لَوْ يَجْعَلُونَكُمْ`, that sense of going back to a prior state would be absent.

### تَرَكَ — to leave in a state

تَرَكَ as a فعل تحويل means to leave something in a particular condition. The implication is that something acted upon the first object, changed it, and departed, leaving it in the new state named by the second object.

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿فَأَصَابَهُ وَابِلٌ فَتَرَكَهُ صَلْدًا﴾
>
> Heavy rain struck it and left it completely barren.

| فَتَرَكَهُ | صَلْدًا |
|---|---|
| فعل + مفعول به أول | مفعول به ثاني |

The image is of soil sitting on a boulder. Greenery grows while the soil stays; when the heavy rain comes it washes the soil off and leaves the rock exposed. `صلدا` means bare, with nothing on top of it.

Taken together, the three verbs mark three different relations to the new state:

| Verb | Relation to the new state |
|---|---|
| صيّر | changes something into a state |
| ردّ | takes it back to a state it held before |
| ترك | leaves it in a state after something has acted on it |

### تَخِذَ and اتَّخَذَ — to take one thing as another

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿وَاتَّخَذَ اللهُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ خَلِيلًا﴾

| اتَّخَذَ | اللهُ | إِبْرَاهِيمَ | خَلِيلًا |
|---|---|---|---|
| فعل تحويل | فاعل | مفعول به أول | مفعول به ثاني |

*And Allah took Ibrahim as a close friend.*

### وَهَبَ and جَعَلَ

Both carry the transformation meaning when they take two objects.

> **Example Box**
>
> وَهَبَنِي اللهُ فِدَاءً لِلْعِلْمِ
>
> May Allah make me a ransom for knowledge, one who gives himself up for it.

> **Core Text**
>
> ﴿فَجَعَلْنَاهُ هَبَاءً مَنْثُورًا﴾
>
> So We made it scattered dust.

The mountains are reduced to dust, and nothing of their former state remains.

**جَعَلَ** requires particular care because it has two distinct uses depending on how many objects follow it:

> **Careful Box**
>
> | Usage | Objects | Meaning |
> |---|---|---|
> | فعل تحويل | two | to make one thing into another |
> | بمعنى خَلَقَ أو أَوْجَدَ | one | to create, bring into existence |
>
> ﴿وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ﴾ — one object only: hearing, sight, and hearts. There is no second object, so this is جَعَلَ in the sense of creation, not transformation.
>
> Some مفسّرون explicitly gloss this جَعَلَ as أَوْجَدَ, to signal to the reader that the two-object تحويل reading is not what is intended here.

## When the same verbs take only one object

These verbs appear in their ordinary transitive meaning, with one object, when the transformation sense is not present:

| Verb | Example | Translation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| تَرَكَ | ﴿إِنِّي تَرَكْتُ مِلَّةَ قَوْمٍ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللهِ﴾ | I have abandoned the way of a people who do not believe in Allah | to abandon or give up, بمعنى هَجَرَ |
| رَدَّ | ﴿ثُمَّ يُرَدُّ إِلَى رَبِّهِ﴾ | then he is returned to his Lord | to be brought back, بمعنى يُصَارُ |
| جَعَلَ | ﴿وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ﴾ | and He made for you hearing, sight, and hearts | to create, بمعنى خَلَقَ أو أَوْجَدَ |

The first of these is Sayyiduna Yusuf speaking to his two companions in prison. Nothing is being left in a new state; the meaning is that he gave up and stopped following that way. Outside the تحويل sense, ترك may also take a single object with no change of state at all, as in `تَرَكْتُ زَيْدًا` or `تَرَكْتُ الْقَلَمَ عَلَى الْمَكْتَبِ`.

> **Rule Box**
>
> The signal is the same as with أفعال القلوب: count the objects. One object means the verb is in its ordinary sense. Two objects, where the second names a state the first is put into, signal فعل تحويل.
