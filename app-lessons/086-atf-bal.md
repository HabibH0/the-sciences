# Lesson 86: أحرف العطف — بَلْ

The eighth of the nine أحرف العطف is بَلْ. Its core meaning is الإِضْراب — turning away from something, moving your attention off of it onto something else.

> **Definition Box**
>
> الثّامِنُ بَلْ، وهي لِلْإِضْرابِ، وهو الإِعْراضُ عَنِ الشَّيْءِ.

الإِعْراض means: the speaker deliberately turns away from what was just said, shifting the focus onto what follows بَلْ instead. Exactly WHAT that turning-away does to the original statement's ruling depends on whether that statement was positive or negative.

## إن سبق بل إيجاب أو أمر: للإبطال

> **Rule Box**
>
> إِنْ سَبَقَ بَلْ إيجابٌ أَوْ أَمْرٌ فَهُوَ لِلْإِبْطالِ، وَهو سَلْبُ الحُكْمِ عَمّا قَبْلَها وَجَعْلِها لِما بَعْدَها.

When بَلْ follows something POSITIVE — either إيجاب (an affirmative statement) or أَمْر (a command) — it works لِلْإِبْطال: it CANCELS/VOIDS the ruling from what came before it, and transfers that same ruling onto what comes after it instead.

> **Example Box**
>
> صُمِ اليَوْمَ بَلْ غَدًا — "Fast today — no, [fast] tomorrow." اليَوْم starts out carrying the command to fast (صُمْ). Then بَلْ arrives and completely strips (سَلْب) that command away from اليوم, handing it instead to غَدًا. The net result: the speaker wants فَسْت on TOMORROW, not today — اليوم's fasting-obligation has been entirely voided (إبطال), as if the speaker corrected themselves mid-sentence.

## وإن سبقها نفي أو نهي: لتقرير حكم ما قبلها

> **Rule Box**
>
> وَإِنْ سَبَقَها نَفْيٌ أَوْ نَهْيٌ فَهُوَ لِتَقْريرِ حُكْمِ ما قَبْلَها وَجَعْلِ ضِدِّها لِما بَعْدَها.

When بَلْ instead follows something NEGATIVE — نَفْي (a negative statement) or نَهْي (a prohibition) — its job flips: it now REAFFIRMS (تقرير) the ruling of what came before it exactly as stated, and assigns the OPPOSITE ruling to what comes after.

> **Example Box**
>
> لا تَصُمِ اليَوْمَ بَلْ غَدًا — "Do not fast today — rather, [fast] tomorrow." اليوم already carries a negative ruling: لا تَصُمْ (don't fast). بَلْ here does NOT cancel that — it CONFIRMS it: yes, indeed, do not fast today, that ruling stands exactly as said. Then بَلْ assigns the OPPOSITE ruling (صُمْ — fasting) to غَدًا. So the net result is the same practical outcome as the إيجاب case (fast tomorrow, not today) — but بَلْ reached it by a different route: by reaffirming a negative rather than cancelling a positive.

> **Careful Box**
>
> The two rules can look like they arrive at similar-sounding sentences (صُمِ اليَوْمَ بَلْ غَدًا vs. لا تَصُمِ اليَوْمَ بَلْ غَدًا both end up meaning "fast tomorrow, not today"), but the grammatical MECHANISM is opposite in each: after a positive, بَلْ VOIDS the first ruling and gives it to the second item (إبطال). After a negative, بَلْ KEEPS the first ruling exactly as it was and gives the OPPOSITE ruling to the second item (تقرير + ضدّ). Always check first whether what precedes بَلْ was positive or negative before deciding what بَلْ is doing to the sentence.
>
> A real-life فقه scenario shows the same mechanism at work in a will (وصيّة): "Give this to Muhammad — no [بَلْ], give it to Zayd" cancels Muhammad's share entirely and hands it to Zayd (إبطال, since "give to Muhammad" was a positive command). But "Do not give this to Fatimah — rather [بَلْ], give it to Zayd" reaffirms that Fatimah gets nothing (تقرير, since "do not give" was already negative) while assigning the OPPOSITE ruling — the giving — to Zayd.

| بَلْ follows... | Function | Effect | Example |
