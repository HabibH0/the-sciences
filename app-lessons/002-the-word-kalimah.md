# Lesson 2: What Is الكلمة?

## The first topic

The introductory section of grammar covers الكلمة first, then الجملة, شبه الجملة, and الكلام, and after that العامل, المعمول, and الإعراب. So the first grammar discussion is about the word:

> **Core Text**
>
> البَابُ الأَوَّلُ فِي الكَلِمَةِ  
> وَهِيَ قَوْلٌ مُفْرَدٌ

So the first definition to learn is:

> **Definition Box**
>
> **الكلمة = قول مفرد**  
> A word is a meaningful single utterance.

The definition has two parts:

| Part | Basic meaning |
|---|---|
| قول | A meaningful utterance |
| مفرد | A single unit, not a construction |

Both parts matter. `قول` excludes sounds that have no recognised meaning. `مفرد` excludes a construction made from separate meaningful parts.

The introduction to grammar begins here because later discussions depend on knowing exactly what counts as one word. Before classifying something as an اسم, فعل, or حرف, the student needs to know what a `كلمة` is.

## What is لفظ?

Before understanding `قول`, first define `لفظ`:

> **Definition Box**
>
> اللَّفْظُ كُلُّ مَا خَرَجَ مِنْ فَمِ الإِنْسَانِ مِنْ صَوْتٍ

`لفظ` is any sound that comes out of a person's mouth. The word itself carries the sense of throwing something out, which is why anything uttered counts, including a cough or the babbling of a small child.

That means every meaningful spoken word is a `لفظ`, but not every `لفظ` is a meaningful word. A random sound can be a `لفظ` without being a `قول`.

> **Careful Box**
>
> `لفظ` is wider than `قول`.  
> A sound may be uttered, but it only becomes `قول` when it points to a meaning.

Grammar books divide `لفظ` into two kinds:

| Term | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| لفظ موضوع | an utterance coined for a meaning, which is what we call `قول` | رَجُلٌ | a man |
| لفظ مهمل | an utterance with no recognised meaning at all | a child's babbling | — |

Only `لفظ موضوع` is studied in grammar. `مهمل` is mentioned so the term is recognised when other books use it.

## What is قول?

Now define `قول`:

> **Definition Box**
>
> القَوْلُ لَفْظٌ دَالٌّ عَلَى مَعْنًى

`قول` is an utterance that points to a meaning.

| Utterance | Translation | Has recognised meaning? | Is it قول? |
|---|---|---|---|
| رَجُلٌ | a man | Yes | Yes |
| زَيْدٌ | Zayd | Yes | Yes |
| a random noise | — | No | No |

> **Example Box**
>
> `رَجُلٌ` is a `قول` because the sound is connected to a known meaning: a man.
>
> The letters are not just noise. The word has been placed for a meaning.

This is why grammar begins with precise definitions. Before analysing جمل, the student must know what counts as a word in the first place.

## Reading the definition

Putting the two parts together, `قول` tells us the word carries meaning, and `مفرد` tells us it is a single unit rather than a construction. So when we say:

> الكلمة قول مفرد

we are saying that a word must be both meaningful and single.

After defining `الكلمة`, the next step is to divide it into its three categories: `اسم`, `فعل`, and `حرف`. Every Arabic word belongs to one of these three.
