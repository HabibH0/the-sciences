# Lesson 56: حرف الجر الزائد — الكاف الزائدة

The fourth and final حرف صِلَة is a زائدة use of الكاف — the same كاف already studied as a حرف جر أصلي, carrying the meaning تشبيه, "resemblance."

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ﴾
>
> There is nothing whatsoever like unto Him.

> **Rule Box**
>
> كَافُ الزَّائِدَةِ تُفِيدُ تَوْكِيدَ التَّشْبِيهِ

This آية describes Allah سبحانه وتعالى, and it does so with two words carrying تشبيه stacked one on the other: كَ, "like," and مِثْل, "resemblance," attached directly to each other — كَمِثْلِهِ, literally "like the resemblance of Him."

## لماذا الكاف هنا زائدة؟

> **Careful Box**
>
> Read الكاف here as حرف جر أصلي, contributing its own full meaning of تشبيه on top of مِثْل, and the sentence says something subtly different from what is intended.

| Reading | الكاف = | Meaning of لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ |
|---|---|---|
| أصلي | full تشبيه, "like" | "Nothing is like the resemblance of Him" — leaves open that Allah has a مِثْل, only denying anything else resembles that مِثْل |
| زائدة | reinforces مِثْل, no independent meaning | "Nothing resembles Him at all" — a single, unambiguous denial that any مِثْل exists in the first place |

Read as أصلي, the sentence only denies that anything resembles a مِثْل, a "likeness," of Allah — leaving an opening: it does not directly deny Allah has a مِثْل in the first place. Read as زائدة, contributing no independent meaning beyond reinforcing مِثْل, the sentence collapses to one unambiguous denial: nothing resembles Him at all.

> **Careful Box**
>
> This is not simply a matter of pure philosophical analysis of what كَ and مِثْل each mean in isolation. Language does not always work word-by-word the way mathematics does — mathematics is the same everywhere with no exceptions, but language is a social convention, and how a phrase is actually used by speakers can diverge from a strict compositional reading of its individual pieces.
>
> A useful parallel is the double negative. Taken word for word, "you ain't know, man" negates a negation and should mean "you are a man" — but in actual usage, the second negative does not cancel the first; it reinforces it, giving an emphasized negative, "you are definitely not a man." Some languages and dialects genuinely use doubled negation this way, as تَأْكِيد rather than cancellation.
>
> لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ works on the same principle: two elements of تشبيه, الكاف and مِثْل, stacked together, do not cancel or double the meaning of resemblance — they intensify the negation already carried by لَيْسَ. The doubling creates emphasis, not a new philosophical claim about a "resemblance of a resemblance."

This is why, even among grammarians who otherwise treat الكاف as حرف جر أصلي in every other setting, there is agreement that at least in constructions like this one, the كاف functions as زائدة — its role here is not to add meaning, but to sharpen the negation already present.

This completes the full set of أحرف صِلَة: الباء الزائدة، مِنْ الزائدة، اللام الزائدة، والكاف الزائدة — four particles, each of which can enter a sentence carrying nothing more than emphasis.
