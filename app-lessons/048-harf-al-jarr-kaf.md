# Lesson 48: حرف الجر الثامن — الْكَاف

الْكَاف is the eighth of the thirteen حروف الجر. Its root meaning is التَّشْبِيه, drawing a likeness between one thing and another.

> **Definition Box**
>
> **مَعْنَى الْكَافِ التَّشْبِيهُ**

## المفعول المطلق

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿رَبِّ ارْحَمْهُمَا كَمَا رَبَّيَانِي صَغِيرًا﴾
>
> My Lord, have mercy upon them as they raised me when I was small.

مفعول مطلق can serve several purposes, one being بَيَان النَّوْع — specifying the manner of the action. الكاف, carrying تشبيه, does exactly this: كَمَا رَبَّيَانِي صَغِيرًا describes the manner of mercy asked for — mercy of the same kind and measure as the mercy they showed in raising him. الكاف ومجرورها functions here as مفعول مطلق غير صريح لِلنَّوْع, standing in for the fuller رَبِّ ارْحَمْهُمَا رَحْمَتَهُمَا إِيَّايَ صَغِيرًا.

## المفعول له، أو التشبيه

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿وَاذْكُرُوهُ كَمَا هَدَاكُمْ﴾
>
> And remember Him, as He has guided you.

> **Careful Box**
>
> **وَيَجُوزُ أَنْ تَكُونَ الْكَافُ لِلتَّشْبِيهِ**
>
> Two readings compete here. Read as مفعول له, كَمَا هَدَاكُمْ gives the reason for the ذِكْر — remember Him because He has guided you, هداية as the cause prompting remembrance. Read as كاف تشبيه instead, carrying الكاف's ordinary root sense, the same wording describes the manner of the ذكر — remember Him in a way that mirrors His guidance of you. Both sit close together in outcome, and telling which a given كَمَا intends is not always obvious without deeper بلاغة work — the choice between لام-like تعليل and كاف-like تشبيه is not arbitrary, even where the surface translation barely shifts between them.

## مسألة: لا يكون مجرورها إلا اسما ظاهرا

> **Rule Box**
>
> **لَا يَكُونُ مَجْرُورُهَا إِلَّا اسْمًا ظَاهِرًا**

Unlike every حرف جر met so far, الكاف never attaches to a ضمير. There is no كَي, كَهُ, or كَنَا — such forms simply do not occur. Where تشبيه is needed with a pronoun, مِثْل takes over instead: مِثْلِي, مِثْلُهُ, and so on. الكاف itself always takes an اسم ظاهر, an explicit noun, never a pronoun.
