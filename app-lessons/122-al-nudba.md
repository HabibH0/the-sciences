# Lesson 122: الندبة

الندبة closes out the نداء section. Where الاستغاثة calls someone for help and نداء التعجب calls something out in amazement, الندبة calls out to something in **grief** — mourning someone who has died, or crying out over pain or loss. It is the most emotionally direct of the four نداء constructions, and grammatically the simplest.

> **Definition Box**
>
> النُّدْبَةُ: إِظْهارُ الحُزْنِ — "displaying grief." النُّدْبَةُ is the نداء-construction used specifically to express sorrow — calling out to a person who has passed away, or to something painful, as a way of voicing that grief out loud.

## أحرف الندبة

> **Rule Box**
>
> تَكونُ النُّدْبَةُ بِحَرْفَيْنِ:
>
> **١. وا** — the dedicated particle of الندبة. This is the safest choice, since وا is never used for anything but expressing grief — there's no risk of the listener mistaking it for an ordinary summons.
>
> **٢. يا** — إِذا أُمِنَ اللَّبْسَ بِالْمُنادَى المَحْضِ, "if confusion with a plain (ordinary) منادى is safely ruled out" — يا may also be used, but only when context makes it unmistakably clear that grief, not an everyday call, is intended.

> **Example Box**
>
> **بِـوا:** وا أَبَتاهُ — "Oh, my father!" — mourning a father who has died or is gravely unwell. There's no possible ambiguity here: وا itself signals grief regardless of context.
>
> **بِـيا (حيث أُمِن اللَّبس):** if a teacher has just passed away and you cry out يا أُسْتاذُ, the surrounding context (his passing) makes it obvious this is grief, not an ordinary call for his attention — so يا is safe to use here even though it's not the dedicated حرف. Likewise, someone gravely ill in hospital: يا فُلانُ, in that setting, is understood as حُزْن.

## إعراب المنادى في الندبة

> **Rule Box**
>
> إِعْرابُ المُنادَى في النُّدْبَةِ **مِثْلُهُ في غَيْرِها** — المنادى in الندبة follows exactly the same إعراب rules laid out for ordinary نداء (منصوب لفظا if مضاف/شبيه بالمضاف/نكرة غير مقصودة؛ مبني في محل نصب if علم مفرد or نكرة مقصودة) — nothing new to learn here.

> **Example Box**
>
> وا زَيْدُ — زَيْدُ مبني على الضم في محل نصب, exactly as it would be in plain نداء (يا زَيْدُ).
>
> وا عَبْدَ اللهِ — عَبْدَ منصوب لفظا because مضاف, exactly as يا عَبْدَ اللهِ would be.

## ما يجوز إلحاقه بآخر المنادى

> **Rule Box**
>
> Two extra letters may optionally be attached to the end of المنادى in الندبة, to heighten or round off the emotional expression:
>
> **١. أَلِفُ النُّدْبَةِ** — an ألف added directly onto the end of المنادى.
>
> **٢. هاءُ السَّكْتِ** — a silent-pause هاء, added *after* ألف الندبة, specifically عِنْدَ الوَقْفِ (when pausing/stopping on the word).

> **Example Box**
>
> **ألف الندبة alone:** وا زَيْدا — the added ألف itself lengthens the cry, intensifying the note of grief (much as drawing out a vowel intensifies an exclamation in any language).
>
> **ألف الندبة + هاء السكت:** وارَأْساهْ — "Oh, my head!" (said by the Prophet ﷺ during his final illness) — و اوَيْلاهْ — "Oh, my ruin!" Both add ألف then a final هاء السكت when pausing on the word, giving the fullest, most drawn-out expression of the grief.

> **Careful Box**
>
> With الندبة, the نداء section comes to a close. Across النداء's four faces — the plain نداء, الاستغاثة and نداء التعجب, and الندبة here — every one of them repurposes the same underlying skeleton (حرف نداء + منادى, standing in for a dropped أَدْعو) toward a different communicative goal: summoning attention, pleading for rescue, marveling in astonishment, or voicing grief. Recognizing which goal is at play is often the only thing that tells these constructions apart on the surface — the grammar itself barely changes.
