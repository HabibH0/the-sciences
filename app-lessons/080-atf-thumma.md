# Lesson 80: أحرف العطف — ثُمَّ

The third of the nine أحرف العطف is ثُمَّ. Like واو, it joins two items together — but unlike واو, it does not stay silent about order. ثُمَّ actively asserts both sequence and a gap in time.

> **Definition Box**
>
> الثّالِثُ ثُمَّ، وهي لِلْجَمْعِ مَعَ التَّرْتيبِ والتَّراخي، نحو: آمَنَ عَليٌّ ثُمَّ عُمَرُ رضي الله عنهما.

> **Rule Box**
>
> ثُمَّ = جمع (joins two items) + تَرْتيب (order — the first item genuinely precedes the second) + تَراخي (a gap or delay between them). This is the opposite of واو's silence on order: where واو leaves order and timing completely open, ثُمَّ commits to both.

> **Example Box**
>
> آمَنَ عَليٌّ ثُمَّ عُمَرُ — "'Ali believed, then 'Umar [believed]." 'Ali (رضي الله عنه) accepted Islam quite early; 'Umar (رضي الله عنه) accepted Islam a good while later. ثُمَّ captures exactly this: 'Umar's إيمان did not merely come after 'Ali's — it came after a real stretch of time (تَراخي), unlike the tight, back-to-back sense that other ordering particles (such as فَ) convey.

## إذا عطفت ثُمَّ جملة على جملة

The plain جمع + ترتيب + تراخي sense above is what ثُمَّ gives when it joins two مفردات (single words/phrases, like عليّ and عمر). But when ثُمَّ instead joins one full جملة (clause) to another, it can carry either of two distinct kinds of ترتيب.

> **Rule Box**
>
> إنْ كانَ ثُمَّ لِعَطْفِ الجُمْلَةِ على الجُمْلَةِ، فَقَدْ يُفيدُ التَّرْتيبَ الزَّمانِيَّ، وَقَدْ يُفيدُ التَّرْتيبَ الذِّكْرِيَّ.

> **Example Box**
>
> **التَّرْتيبُ الزَّمانِيُّ** (ordinary chronological order — this is ثُمَّ's default, literal sense): ﴿وَكُنْتُمْ أَمْواتًا فَأَحْياكُمْ ثُمَّ يُميتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْييكُمْ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعونَ﴾ — "You were dead and He gave you life; then He will cause you to die, then bring you to life again, then to Him you will be returned." Four real stages in real chronological sequence: non-existence → life → death → resurrection → the Return — each ثُمَّ marking a genuine gap in time before the next stage begins.
>
> **التَّرْتيبُ الذِّكْرِيُّ** (rhetorical order — "furthermore," not literal chronology): ﴿فَإِلَيْنا مَرْجِعُهُمْ ثُمَّ اللَّهُ شَهيدٌ عَلٰى ما يَفْعَلونَ﴾ — "To Us is their return; furthermore, Allah is witness over what they do." Allah's watching over their deeds does not literally happen "after" their return to Him in time — He is witness all along. Here ثُمَّ instead signals that the second clause is a MORE IMPORTANT, higher-ranked fact than the first (الجُمْلَةُ المَعْطوفَةُ بِها أَعْلٰى رُتْبَةً مِنَ المَعْطوفَةِ عَلَيْها) — "and what's more..." — not that it chronologically follows it.

> **Careful Box**
>
> Do not assume every ثُمَّ between two clauses reports literal chronological events. Ask first whether a real time-gap between the two clauses actually makes sense in context. If it does — as with life, death, and resurrection — read ثُمَّ as تَرْتيب زَمانِيّ. If a literal time-reading would be senseless or redundant (as with Allah's witnessing, which is not a later event but an ongoing, ever-present reality) — read ثُمَّ as تَرْتيب ذِكْرِيّ, i.e. "furthermore" / "and what is more," ranking the second clause as weightier than the first rather than placing it later in time.

## Summary table

| Sense of ثُمَّ | What it asserts | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Basic (joining مفردات) | جمع + تَرْتيب + تَراخي (order with a real time-gap) | آمَنَ عَليٌّ ثُمَّ عُمَرُ |
| تَرْتيب زَمانِيّ (joining جمل) | Literal chronological sequence, real events in real time | وَكُنْتُمْ أَمْواتًا فَأَحْياكُمْ ثُمَّ يُميتُكُمْ ثُمَّ يُحْييكُمْ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ تُرْجَعونَ |
| تَرْتيب ذِكْرِيّ (joining جمل) | Rhetorical "furthermore" — second clause outranks the first, no literal time-gap | فَإِلَيْنا مَرْجِعُهُمْ ثُمَّ اللَّهُ شَهيدٌ عَلٰى ما يَفْعَلونَ |
