# Lesson 25: Omission Rules for أفعال القلوب

## Two ways of omitting objects

A `فعل قلب` takes its two objects from an original `مبتدأ` and `خبر`. Those are the `عمد` of the sentence, the parts without which it is not complete, which is why the objects of a `فعل قلب` cannot simply be dropped with nothing left to recover them from.

When they are not stated, the situation falls into one of two categories. The two terms look and sound alike, but they are opposites in what they permit.

### اختصار — permitted

اختصار is shortening: both objects are omitted, but the evidence for what is missing exists in the surrounding words.

> **Core Text**
>
> يَجُوزُ الِاخْتِصَارُ فِي مَفْعُولَيْ أَفْعَالِ الْقُلُوبِ، وَهُوَ الْحَذْفُ بِدَلِيلٍ عَلَى الْمَحْذُوفِ الْمَنْوِيِّ

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿أَيْنَ شُرَكَاؤُكُمُ الَّذِينَ كُنْتُمْ تَزْعُمُونَ﴾
>
> زَعَمَ takes two objects. Neither is stated. The understood meaning is: تَزْعُمُونَهُمْ شُرَكَائِي — you claimed them to be My partners. The verse itself supplies the evidence. Both objects are present in the mind even though not in the text.

### اقتصار — not permitted

اقتصار is sufficing with one object: only one of the two objects is omitted, and there is no evidence in the context for what the missing object was.

> **Core Text**
>
> وَلَا يَجُوزُ الِاقْتِصَارُ فِي حَذْفِ أَحَدِ مَفْعُولَيْ أَفْعَالِ الْقُلُوبِ، وَهُوَ الْحَذْفُ بِغَيْرِ دَلِيلٍ عَلَى الْمَحْذُوفِ الْمَنْوِيِّ

> **Example Box**
>
> ظَنَنْتُهُ, with no second object and no context, is not valid as a فعل قلب.
>
> The listener has no way to know what was being judged about him. Without that second object, the sentence shifts to its other available meaning: *I suspected him*, بمعنى اتهمته. The فعل القلب reading is lost.

> **Careful Box**
>
> What decides the matter is whether there is evidence for the omitted meaning, not how many objects were dropped. Omitting both with evidence (اختصار) is allowed. Omitting one without evidence (اقتصار) is not.

## These verbs only take two objects as أفعال القلوب

The verbs listed under أفعال القلوب and أفعال التحويل are not exclusively those types of verbs. Many of them have other meanings in which they are ordinary transitive verbs taking one object. The two-object construction only applies when the verb is being used with the meaning of inner knowledge or transformation.

> **Core Text**
>
> وَهَذِهِ الْأَفْعَالُ لَا تَتَعَدَّى إِلَى مَفْعُولَيْنِ إِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ مِنْ أَفْعَالِ الْقُلُوبِ وَالتَّحْوِيلِ

| Verb | One object (other meaning) | Two objects (فعل قلب) |
|---|---|---|
| عَلِمَ | ﴿لَا تَعْلَمُونَ شَيْئًا﴾, you knew nothing — بمعنى عَرَفَ | عَلِمْتُ زيداً كريماً, I knew Zayd to be noble |
| اتَّخَذَ | ﴿لَوْ شِئْتَ لَاتَّخَذْتَ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا﴾, had you wished you could have sought a wage — بمعنى طَلَبَ | ﴿وَاتَّخَذَ اللهُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ خَلِيلًا﴾, and Allah took Ibrahim as a close friend |
| أَلْفَى | ﴿أَلْفَيَا سَيِّدَهَا لَدَى الْبَابِ﴾, they came upon her master at the door — بمعنى صَادَفَ | أَلْفَيْتُه عالماً, I found him to be learned |
| وَجَدَ | وجدتُ الضالةَ, I found the lost thing — بمعنى أصابه وأخذه | ﴿وَوَجَدَكَ ضَالًّا﴾, He found you searching for the truth |
| ظَنَّ | ظننتُه, I suspected him — بمعنى اتَّهَمَه | ظننتُ زيداً قائماً, I considered Zayd to be standing |
| رَأَى | رأيتُ زيداً, I saw Zayd — بمعنى أبصرته, رؤية بصرية | رأيتُ زيداً عالماً, I considered Zayd a scholar |

In ﴿أَلْفَيَا سَيِّدَهَا لَدَى الْبَابِ﴾, `سَيِّدَهَا` is the single `مفعول به` and `لَدَى الْبَابِ` is a `ظرف` functioning as `مفعول فيه`. They ran to the door and came across her master there; nothing is being found to be in a particular state. This is the point on which the earlier اختلاف about `ألفى` turns.

Because `رأى` runs in both directions, it is the verb where the label is normally stated in analysis: `رأى القلبية` for the inner meaning, `رأى الجارحة` for physical sight.

> **Careful Box**
>
> When parsing a verb in a text, the number of objects present is the primary signal. One object means the verb is functioning in its ordinary sense. Two objects — where the second completes a predication begun by the first — signals فعل قلب or فعل تحويل. The distinction is not in the verb itself but in how it is being used.
