# Lesson 101: حرفا الاستقبال

حَرْفا الِاسْتِقْبال are the two particles that "futurize" a فِعْل مُضارِع: السِّين and سَوْفَ. Ordinarily a فعل مضارع can carry either a present or a future sense, but once either of these two particles attaches to it, its meaning is locked into the future. The two particles are not interchangeable, though — each pairs with a different negative counterpart and marks a different distance of future time.

> **Definition Box**
>
> القسم الثّالِثَ عَشَرَ: حَرْفا الِاسْتِقْبالِ. الْأَوَّلُ السّينُ، وهي لِإِثْباتِ المُسْتَقْبَلِ القَريبِ في مُقابَلَةِ لا، نحو: ﴿سَيَقولُ السُّفَهاءُ مِنَ النّاسِ﴾. مَسْأَلَةٌ: تُسَمّى السّينُ حَرْفَ تَنْفيسٍ. الثّاني سَوْفَ، وهي لِإِثْباتِ المُسْتَقْبَلِ البَعيدِ في مُقابَلَةِ لَنْ، نحو: ﴿سَوْفَ تَعْلَمونَ﴾.

## السّين: المستقبل القريب، حرف تنفيس

> **Rule Box**
>
> السّينُ لِإِثْباتِ المُسْتَقْبَلِ القَريبِ، في مُقابَلَةِ لا. وَتُسَمّى حَرْفَ تَنْفيسٍ.

السين enters on a فعل مضارع and confirms that its action belongs to the NEAR future — something that will happen soon. Its negative counterpart is لا, itself used for near-future negation, so the pair السين/لا both sit on the "near" side of the future. Because of this sense of "opening up" the verb into the near future, السين is named حَرْف تَنْفيس — a particle of relief or breathing space (from نَفَّسَ, to give room).

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿سَيَقولُ السُّفَهاءُ مِنَ النّاسِ﴾ — "The foolish among the people will soon say..." — referring to the objections that would soon arise over the change of the قِبْلَة. The السين marks this as imminent, not far off.
>
> Illustrative sentence: سَيَشِبُّ الْغُلامُ — "The boy will soon grow into a young man" — again a near-future change.

## سَوْفَ: المستقبل البعيد، حرف تسويف

> **Rule Box**
>
> سَوْفَ لِإِثْباتِ المُسْتَقْبَلِ البَعيدِ، في مُقابَلَةِ لَنْ. وَتُسَمّى حَرْفَ تَسْويفٍ.

سَوْفَ also enters on a فعل مضارع, but confirms that its action belongs to the FAR future. Its negative counterpart is لَنْ, itself used for far-future/categorical negation, so the pair سَوْفَ/لَنْ both sit on the "far" side. سَوْفَ takes its name, حَرْف تَسْويف, from the verb سَوَّفَ ("to procrastinate, to keep putting off") — the same root behind المُسَوِّفون, the description of people who constantly say "I'll do it later" and never actually do it.

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿سَوْفَ تَعْلَمونَ﴾ — "Soon you shall come to know..." — not an immediate reckoning, but one that will arrive in its due time; a warning against being deceived into thinking the delay means escape.
>
> Illustrative sentence: وَسَوْفَ يَشيخُ الفَتٰى — "And after a long time [some thirty years or so], the young man will become an old man." Set beside the السين example (سَيَشِبُّ الْغُلامُ وَسَوْفَ يَشيخُ الفَتٰى), the contrast is vivid: turning from boy to young man is comparatively near; growing old is comparatively far.

> **Careful Box**
>
> Keep the two pairings straight: السين ↔ لا (both قريب), سَوْفَ ↔ لَنْ (both بَعيد). The "nearness" and "farness" are relative to one another rather than fixed spans of time — السين's future is simply nearer than سَوْفَ's.
