# Lesson 3: مفرد and مركب

## مفرد in the definition of الكلمة

Define `مفرد` like this:

> **Definition Box**
>
> المُفْرَدُ مَا لا تَدُلُّ أَجْزَاؤُهُ عَلَى مَعْنًى

`مفرد` is something whose parts do not separately point to meanings.

> **Example Box**
>
> `رَجُلٌ` means "a man".  
> But `ر`, `ج`, and `ل` do not each carry a separate part of that meaning.
>
> The whole word carries the meaning together, so `رجل` is `مفرد`.

## Visual tarkeeb: رجل

| ر | ج | ل | Whole word |
|---|---|---|---|
| no separate meaning | no separate meaning | no separate meaning | رَجُلٌ = man |

The meaning belongs to the whole word, not to the separate letters.

The point is not whether the word has several letters. Most words have several letters. The question is whether those parts independently carry parts of the meaning.

## مركب

The opposite of `مفرد` is `مركب`.

> **Definition Box**
>
> المُرَكَّبُ مَا تَدُلُّ أَجْزَاؤُهُ عَلَى مَعْنًى

`مركب` is a construction whose parts do point to meanings.

> **Example Box**
>
> `كِتَابُ الوَلَدِ`
>
> `كتاب` has a meaning.  
> `الولد` has a meaning.  
> The construction is built from meaningful parts, so it is `مركب`.

## Visual tarkeeb: كتاب الولد

| كتاب | الولد | Whole construction |
|---|---|---|
| book | the boy | the boy's book |

Because each part has its own meaning, this is `مركب`.

So the difference between `مفرد` and `مركب` is about how meaning is distributed. In `رجل`, the meaning belongs to the whole word. In `كتاب الولد`, the meaning is built from two meaningful words.

## ناقص and تام

A `مركب` is either incomplete or complete.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| مركب ناقص | a construction that does not make a complete sentence, that is, a phrase |
| مركب تام | a construction that makes a complete جملة, a full sentence |

A `مركب ناقص` covers the phrase types already met in earlier study, such as `مضاف` with `مضاف إليه`, and `منعوت` with `نعت`. `كتاب الولد` is of this kind: `كتاب` is `مضاف` and `الولد` is `مضاف إليه`, and the two together still do not form a sentence.

## Two uses of مفرد

`مفرد` does not always mean the same thing in every chapter.

| Context | Meaning | Opposite |
|---|---|---|
| Definition of الكلمة | not مركب | مركب |
| Number | singular | مثنى / جمع |

> **Careful Box**
>
> `رِجَالٌ` is one word, so it is `مفرد` when discussing whether something is a single word or a construction.
>
> But it is `جمع` when discussing number, because it means men.

This prevents a common confusion: `مفرد` in grammar may refer to composition, not number. Always ask which discussion you are in.

In the discussion of number, the same categories carry more than one name. `مفرد` is also called `موحد`, `مثنى` is also called `تثنية`, and `جمع` is also called `مجموع`.

## تركيب المزج and تركيب العطف

Some compounds need a separate note, because their parts once had meanings but the finished expression no longer works that way.

| Type | Example | Translation | How it is treated |
|---|---|---|---|
| تركيب المزج | بَعْلَبَكَّ | Baalbek | treated as `مفرد` in this discussion |
| تركيب العدد from 11 to 19 | أَحَدَ عَشَرَ | eleven | treated as تركيب مزج |
| تركيب العطف in numbers | أَحَدٌ وَعِشْرُونَ | twenty-one | treated as تركيب عطف because of the و |

`بعلبك` is a useful example. Its original parts may have carried meanings, but as a proper name it now functions as one combined unit. For the definition of `الكلمة`, the concern is how the expression functions in speech now.
