# Lesson 76: حروف تنصب الفعل المضارع — إِذَنْ

The fourth and final particle in this group is إِذَنْ. Unlike أنْ، كَيْ، ولَنْ, it isn't used to open a new idea — it exists specifically to respond to something someone else has just said.

> **Rule Box**
>
> الرّابِعُ إِذَنْ، وهي لِلْجَوابِ.

> **Example Box**
>
> Someone says أُطيعُ اللَّهَ ("I will obey Allah"), and you reply: إِذَنْ تَدْخُلَ الجَنَّةَ — "Then [in that case] you will enter Paradise." إِذَنْ picks up directly on what the other person just said and draws out its consequence — that is its whole function: لِلْجَوابِ, "for answering."

## هل إذن حرف أم اسم؟

> **Careful Box**
>
> الجُمْهورُ على أَنَّهُ حَرْفٌ, though a minority position treats it as an اسم. The minority reasoning: أَصْلُهُ إِذْ الشَّرْطِيَّةُ — إِذَنْ is said to originate from إِذْ (a ظرف, "when/since," itself an اسم), with its شرط (the conditional clause that would normally follow إِذْ) dropped, and a تنوين عوض (compensating تنوين) added in its place to make up for what was lost. On that view, إِذَنْ تَدْخُلَ الجَنَّةَ unpacks as إِذا تُطِعِ اللَّهَ تَدْخُلَ الجَنَّةَ — "when/if you obey Allah, you will enter Paradise" — with the إذا-clause abbreviated all the way down to إذن. The جمهور reject this etymology in practice and simply treat إِذَنْ as its own حرف, but the historical derivation is worth knowing, since it explains why some books label it اسم.

## شرائط عمل إذن

إِذَنْ does not govern (نصب الفعل المضارع) automatically — it needs three conditions to line up.

> **Rule Box**
>
> وَلا تَعْمَلُ إِذَنْ إِلّا بِشُروطٍ ثَلاثٍ: أَنْ تَقَعَ في صَدْرِ الكَلامِ، وَأَنْ يَلِيَها فِعْلٌ مُسْتَقْبَلٌ، وَأَلّا يُفْصَلَ بَيْنَها وَبَيْنَ الفِعْلِ.

**Condition one — إِذَنْ must open the sentence (صدر الكلام).** If it appears mid-sentence instead, it does not govern.

> **Example Box**
>
> أَنْتَ إِذَنْ تَدْخُلُ الجَنَّةَ — تَدْخُلُ stays مرفوع here; إِذَنْ has been pushed out of صدر الكلام by أَنْتَ, so it no longer governs.

> **Careful Box**
>
> If إِذَنْ is preceded only by وَ or فَ — and by nothing else — both options remain open (الوَجْهانِ جائزان): إِذَنْ may still govern, or it may not. In practice, إِهْمال (not governing) is by far the more common choice, and this is exactly the pattern the Qur'an follows: ﴿فَإِذًا لا يُؤْتونَ النّاسَ نَقيرًا﴾ — "then they would not give people so much as the speck on a date stone" — يُؤْتونَ keeps its ن (مرفوع, not منصوب), even with فَ directly before إِذَنْ. Since a bare وَ or فَ doesn't count as breaking صدر الكلام the way a full word like أَنْتَ does, إِذَنْ retains the possibility of governing here — the Qur'an's own usage simply favors the إهمال option.

**Condition two — a مستقبل (future-referring) فعل must follow it directly.**

**Condition three — nothing may separate إِذَنْ from that فعل**, with one single exception: a قَسَم (oath) is allowed to intervene.

> **Example Box**
>
> إِذَنْ أَنا أُكْرِمُكَ — أُكْرِمُ stays مرفوع; أَنا has separated إِذَنْ from its فعل, so إِذَنْ becomes مُهْمَل.
>
> إِذَنْ وَاللَّهِ أُكْرِمُكَ — أُكْرِمَ is منصوب here despite وَاللَّهِ intervening, because a قَسَم is the one recognized exception to condition three.

> **Careful Box**
>
> Even when all three conditions are fully met, some grammarians still allow the speaker to leave إِذَنْ مُهْمَل by choice — بَعْضُهُمْ يُهْمِلُها مَعَ اِسْتيفائِها شُروطَها فَلا يَعْمَلُ أَلْبَتَّةَ. In other words, satisfying the three شروط makes إعمال possible, but never strictly obligatory.

## كتابة إذن

> **Rule Box**
>
> تُكْتَبُ إِذَنْ في المُصْحَفِ بِالْأَلِفِ، نحو: ﴿فَإِذًا﴾.

Despite normally being spelled with a final نون (إِذَنْ), the رسم عثماني (the Uthmanic script of the muṣḥaf) always writes this word with a final أَلِف instead — فَإِذًا, not فَإِذَنْ. Since the word is pronounced with تنوين either way, and — as seen above — the Qur'an consistently uses إِذَنْ in its مُهْمَلَة (non-governing) form rather than governing a منصوب فعل, the muṣḥaf's spelling convention causes no ambiguity in how the word is actually read or understood.
