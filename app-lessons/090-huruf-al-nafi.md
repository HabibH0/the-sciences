# Lesson 90: أحرف الجواب — الضرب الثاني: حرفا النفي

The second ضرب of أحرف الجواب is حَرْفا النَّفْي — the two negative response particles: لا and كَلّا. لا is simple; كَلّا carries far more nuance and is the main focus of this lesson.

> **Definition Box**
>
> الضَّرْبُ الثّاني: حَرْفا النَّفْي. الْأَوَّلُ لا. الثّاني كَلّا لِلزَّجْرِ، وهو رَدٌّ لِما قَبْلَهُ وَإِثْباتٌ لِما بَعْدَهُ.

## الأول: لا

> **Example Box**
>
> هَلْ تُبْغِضُ الصَّحابَةَ؟ — لا — "Do you hate the Companions? — No." لا simply negates plainly — it carries no extra emotional or rhetorical charge; it is pure information.

## الثاني: كَلّا — المعنى الأصلي: الزجر والردع

> **Rule Box**
>
> كَلّا حَرْفُ رَدْعٍ وَزَجْرٍ عَنْ مَضْمونِ كَلامٍ سابِقٍ، وهو رَدٌّ لِما قَبْلَهُ وَإِثْباتٌ لِما بَعْدَهُ.

Unlike the flat, neutral لا, كَلّا carries real force: الزَّجْر means to REBUKE or SCOLD, not merely to disagree. When someone says كَلّا, they are not just saying "no" — they are firmly rejecting what was just said, often with an implicit "how dare you say that."

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿قالَ رَبِّ ارْجِعونِ ۝ لَعَلّي أَعْمَلُ صالِحًا فيما تَرَكْتُ كَلّا﴾ — "He will say: My Lord, send me back, that I might do righteousness in that which I left behind. No!" This is the disbeliever in the Hereafter, begging for a second chance. كَلّا here is Allah's (or the angels') sharp rebuke: that plea will never be granted — the request itself is being firmly, scoldingly refused, not just declined.

> **Rule Box**
>
> والغالِبُ أَنْ تَكونَ كَلّا مُتْبَعَةً بِكَلامٍ بَعْدَها — فَلا يُعْهَدُ في كَلامِ العَرَبِ أَنْ يَقولَ قائِلٌ في رَدِّ كَلامٍ: كَلّا، ثُمَّ يَسْكُتُ.

Unlike نَعَمْ, لا, or بَلٰى, which can all stand alone as a complete one-word answer, كَلّا is almost always followed by MORE speech — the Arabs simply don't say كَلّا and then fall silent. Compare: هَلْ فَهِمْتَ؟ — نَعَمْ [stop] is perfectly natural; but كَلّا is expected to be followed by further explanation of what exactly is being rebuked.

> **Careful Box**
>
> لَمْ يَرِدْ كَلِمَةُ كَلّا إِلّا في سورَةٍ مَكِّيَّةٍ — the word كَلّا occurs ONLY in Makkan sūrahs of the Qur'an, never in a Madinan one. This is a useful textual marker, not a coincidence — it reflects the confrontational, corrective tone often needed when addressing the disbelief and denial that characterized the Makkan period.

## المعاني الفرعية (المجازية) لكلّا: التصديق والاستفتاح

> **Rule Box**
>
> وَقَدْ تَأْتي كَلّا لِغَيْرِ الزَّجْرِ والرَّدْعِ: لِلتَّصْديقِ لِما قَبْلَهُ (بِمَعْنٰى أَجَلْ وَإِي)، أَوْ لِلِاسْتِفْتاحِ (بِمَعْنٰى أَلا).

Beyond its primary (حَقيقة) sense of rebuke, كَلّا can carry two secondary (مَجاز) meanings depending on context: confirming what preceded it (تَصْديق), or simply opening/introducing what follows (اسْتِفْتاح) — in neither of these does it carry any rebuke at all.

> **Example Box**
>
> **التصديق (بمعنى أجل/إي — "yes indeed"):** ﴿وَما هِيَ إِلّا ذِكْرٰى لِلْبَشَرِ ۝ كَلّا وَالْقَمَرِ﴾ — "It is but a reminder to mankind. Indeed, by the moon..." There is nothing here to rebuke — Allah is simply CONFIRMING the preceding statement (that the Qur'an is a reminder) before swearing an oath by the moon. كَلّا here functions like أَجَلْ or إي: "yes, truly."
>
> **الاستفتاح (بمعنى ألا — simply opening a new, emphatic statement):** ﴿عَلَّمَ الْإِنسانَ ما لَمْ يَعْلَمْ ۝ كَلّا إِنَّ الْإِنْسانَ لَيَطْغٰى﴾ — "He taught man what he did not know. Indeed! Man truly transgresses." This كَلّا cannot possibly be تَصْديق (confirming what came right before), لِتَأَخُّرِ نُزولِهِ — because, historically, this second آية was revealed much LATER than the first, with a real gap in revelation between them; there is nothing immediately prior for it to "confirm." Instead it functions like أَلا — an opening word of emphasis, drawing attention to what FOLLOWS it, much as أَلا often does.

> **Careful Box**
>
> Telling تصديق and استفتاح apart, and telling either apart from زجر, is not always obvious from the words alone — context and (for Qur'anic examples) the history of revelation matter. The test used for the علّم الإنسان example specifically is chronological: since a real gap in revelation separates the two verses, كَلّا cannot be "confirming" a sentence that, historically, was not even revealed alongside it yet — ruling out تصديق and supporting استفتاح instead.

## الوقف: أين تتوقف القراءة؟

> **Rule Box**
>
> إِذا كانَتْ كَلّا لِلزَّجْرِ، يَقَعُ الوَقْفُ عَلَيْها، والِابْتِداءُ بِما بَعْدَها. وَإِذا كانَتْ لِلتَّصْديقِ، يَقَعُ الوَقْفُ على ما قَبْلَها، والِابْتِداءُ بِها.

Where كَلّا falls in a recitation pause (وَقْف) depends entirely on which of its meanings is intended: for زَجْر, you pause AFTER كَلّا, then begin fresh with what follows (لَعَلّي أَعْمَلُ صالِحًا فيما تَرَكْتُ ... [pause] ... كَلّا. [pause] ...). For تَصْديق, the pause instead falls BEFORE كَلّا, and you begin the next portion WITH كَلّا attached to what follows it (...لِلْبَشَرِ. [pause] كَلّا وَالْقَمَرِ...).

> **Careful Box**
>
> When كَلّا is genuinely ambiguous between the two meanings, الرَّدْعُ (rebuke) is preferred by default, لِأَنَّهُ أَصْلٌ — because rebuke is كَلّا's PRIMARY, original meaning, and the other senses (تصديق، استفتاح) are secondary/metaphorical extensions (مَجاز). الحَقيقَةُ أَوْلٰى مِنَ المَجازِ — the literal/original meaning always takes precedence over a figurative one when both are possible readings. ﴿أَطَّلَعَ الْغَيْبَ أَمِ اتَّخَذَ عِنْدَ الرَّحْمٰنِ عَهْدًا ۝ كَلّا سَنَكْتُبُ ما يَقولُ﴾ illustrates this: كَلّا here could arguably work either way, but is read as رَدْع by default.

## معنى فرعي مرجوح: حَقًّا

> **Rule Box**
>
> وَعِنْدَ البَعْضِ كَلّا بِمَعْنٰى حَقًّا، وَهٰذا مَرْجوحٌ.

A minority opinion holds that كَلّا can simply mean حَقًّا ("truly/certainly") in some places. This view is considered weak (مَرْجوح) for a grammatical reason: after the word حَقًّا, what follows should normally be مَفْتوحَة (in the فتحة case-ending), but the Qur'anic text after كَلّا in the relevant verses is مَكْسورَة (in the كسرة case-ending) — a mismatch that argues against reading كَلّا as a simple synonym of حَقًّا.

## قد يكون المردود مقدّرًا

> **Careful Box**
>
> Even within the primary زَجْر/رَدّ meaning, what is being "rebuked" (المَرْدود) is not always an explicit sentence spoken just before كَلّا — it can be مُقَدَّر (an implied/hidden statement, not literally present in the text). ﴿عَلَّمَ الْإِنسانَ ما لَمْ يَعْلَمْ ۝ كَلّا إِنَّ الْإِنْسانَ لَيَطْغٰى﴾ can be read this way too: كَلّا is not rebuking the sentence "He taught man what he did not know" itself (that statement is simply true) — it is rebuking an UNSTATED, implied claim, something like "[so man should be grateful]" — which كَلّا then firmly rejects: NO — despite all that Allah has given him, man transgresses instead. Recognizing a مُقَدَّر (hidden) مَرْدود is part of correctly reading كَلّا in context, especially when no explicit prior claim seems to be directly negated.
