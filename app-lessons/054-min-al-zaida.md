# Lesson 54: حرف الجر الزائد — مِنْ الزائدة

The second of the أحرف صِلَة is a زائدة use of مِنْ — the same مِنْ already studied as the second of the thirteen حروف الجر الأصلية, and again, separately, in its تبعيضية use under حرف الجر الشبيه بالزائد. Here it appears a third time, carrying no meaning of its own beyond تأكيد.

> **Rule Box**
>
> تَقَعُ مِنَ الزَّائِدَةُ عَلَى تَمْيِيزِ كَمِ الخَبَرِيَّةِ

The first position is on the تمييز of كَمِ الخَبَرِيَّة, already met earlier — the exclamatory كَمْ meaning "how many!":

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿كَمْ آتَيْنَاهُمْ مِنْ آيَةٍ بَيِّنَةٍ﴾
>
> How many a clear sign We gave them!

آيَةٍ is تمييز of كَمْ, and the زائدة مِنْ attaches directly to it.

> **Careful Box**
>
> Not everyone reads this مِنْ as زائدة. Some hold it is أصلية here, with the شِبْه الجملة مِنْ آيَةٍ itself functioning as تمييز — a تمييز غَيْر صَرِيح, "non-explicit," rather than the plain noun تمييز would ordinarily be. Either analysis reaches the same sentence; they differ only on whether مِنْ is contributing its own governing force or simply riding along for emphasis.

## في كلام غير موجب

> **Rule Box**
>
> تَقَعُ مِنَ الزَّائِدَةُ فِي كَلَامٍ غَيْرِ مُوجَبٍ، فِي سِيَاقِ النَّفْيِ بِأَدَاةٍ مِنْ أَدَوَاتِ النَّفْيِ، أَوْ بِاسْتِفْهَامٍ بِمَعْنَى النَّفْيِ، أَوْ فِي سِيَاقِ اسْتِفْهَامٍ بِهَلْ

Beyond تمييز كَمْ, الزائدة مِنْ has three further positions, all sharing one condition: the sentence must be كَلَامٌ غَيْرُ مُوجَبٍ, "a non-affirmative sentence."

| Context | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| Plain negation (أداة نفي) | ﴿مَا جَاءَنَا مِنْ بَشِيرٍ وَلَا نَذِيرٍ﴾ | No bearer of good news came to us, nor any warner |
| Question = rhetorical negation | ﴿هَلْ مِنْ خَالِقٍ غَيْرُ اللَّهِ﴾ | Is there any creator other than Allah? |
| Genuine question with هَلْ | ﴿هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ﴾ | Is there any more? |

In the second example, هَلْ is not really asking for information — it means لَا خَالِقَ غَيْرُ اللَّهِ, "there is no creator besides Allah." Because the question carries the meaning of نفي, it licenses الزائدة مِنْ exactly as a true negation would.

The third case is subtler: ﴿هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ﴾ is جهنم's own reply on the Day of Judgment when asked if it is full — and here هَلْ really is a genuine question, not a rhetorical denial. Even so, الزائدة مِنْ is licensed, because the underlying structure — نكرة inside a هَلْ-clause — is close enough to the negation cases to admit the same particle.

> **Careful Box**
>
> مَزِيدٍ here is مُبْتَدَأٌ مَرْفُوعٌ مَحَلًّا despite its apparent جر — the زائدة مِنْ never changes the محلي role of what it attaches to. Its خَبَر is مَحْذُوف, dropped, with تقدير مَوْجُودٌ: "is there [any] more, existing [beyond what is already here]?"

### لماذا تدخل الزائدة مِنْ في هذه السياقات؟

> **Careful Box**
>
> A نكرة inside a negative sentence already conveys عُموم, generality, by default — مَا جَاءَنَا رَجُلٌ, "no man came to us," is naturally read as "not a single man came." But this default reading carries a secondary possibility, احتمال الإفراد: the sentence could instead be denying only that one man came, leaving open that two or more did — "not one man came, but two." الزائدة مِنْ exists precisely to close off that second possibility. مَا جَاءَنَا مِنْ رَجُلٍ removes any doubt: it is explicitly, unambiguously عموم — nobody at all. This is why الزائدة مِنْ here is sometimes named مِنْ لِلتَّنْصِيصِ عَلَى العُمُومِ, "مِنْ that makes the generality explicit," or مِنْ لِتَأْكِيدِ التَّنْصِيصِ عَلَى العُمُومِ, "مِنْ that reinforces that explicitness."

### هل تقع في كلام موجب؟

> **Careful Box**
>
> عِنْدَ الكُوفِيِّينَ وَالأَخْفَشِ, الزائدة مِنْ can also appear in كَلَام مُوجَب, an affirmative sentence — against the general rule confining it to negative and interrogative contexts. Their example: ﴿اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاتَّقُوهُ وَأَطِيعُونِ يَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ مِنْ ذُنُوبِكُمْ﴾, "worship Allah, fear Him, and obey me — He will forgive you مِنْ ذُنُوبِكُمْ." Read مِنْ as زائدة here, and the verse promises forgiveness of all your sins, not merely some — supported by ﴿إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا﴾, "indeed Allah forgives all sins," and by the حديث «أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّ الْإِسْلَامَ يَهْدِمُ مَا كَانَ قَبْلَهُ», "do you not know that Islam wipes out what came before it," reported by مسلم.

| View | مِنْ ذُنُوبِكُمْ read as | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| الكوفيون والأخفش | زائدة | Allah forgives all of your sins |
| الجمهور | تبعيضية (partitive) | Allah forgives some of your sins — specifically حقوق الله |

الجمهور reject the زائدة reading and keep مِنْ as مِنْ التَّبْعِيضِيَّة, "partitive مِنْ" — forgiveness of some sins, not all. Their answer: sins divide into two categories. حُقُوق اللَّه — obligations owed to Allah alone, like شرك — are wiped out entirely upon entering Islam, matching the الإسلام يهدم ما قبله حديث. حُقُوق العِبَاد — obligations owed to other people, like unpaid debts, blood money, or unresolved ربا transactions — are not automatically erased; they remain to be settled with the people owed. On this reading, مِنْ ذُنُوبِكُمْ carves out this second category: Allah forgives your sins insofar as they are His right to forgive, while حقوق العباد are excluded, لِإِخْرَاجِ حُقُوقِ العِبَادِ, "to exclude the rights of [His] servants," from that forgiveness — the verse never claimed all sins are wiped out unconditionally, only those purely between the servant and Allah.
