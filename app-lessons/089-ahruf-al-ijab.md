# Lesson 89: أحرف الجواب — الضرب الأول: أحرف الإيجاب

Having finished أحرف العطف entirely, a new قسم begins: أَحْرُفُ الجَواب — "response particles," the words used to answer a question or statement with a single word. These fall into two ضربان (types): أَحْرُفُ الإيجاب (affirmative response particles) and حَرْفا النَّفْي (the two negative response particles). This lesson covers the first type in full: the six أحرف الإيجاب.

> **Definition Box**
>
> القِسمُ الثّاني: أَحْرُفُ الجَوابِ، وهي ضَرْبانِ: الضَّرْبُ الْأَوَّلُ أَحْرُفُ الإيجابِ، وهي سِتَّةٌ: نَعَمْ، بَلٰى، إي، أَجَلْ، إِنَّهْ، جَيْرِ.

## الأول: نَعَمْ

> **Rule Box**
>
> نَعَمْ تُفيدُ الْإِثْباتَ في الجَوابِ عَنِ السُّؤالِ المُثْبَتِ، والنَّفْيَ في الجَوابِ عَنِ السُّؤالِ المَنْفِيِّ.

نَعَمْ ("yes") is the ordinary, general-purpose affirmative response. Critically, its meaning depends entirely on whether the QUESTION it answers was itself phrased positively or negatively — نَعَمْ simply CONFIRMS whatever the question already assumed, in either direction.

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿فَهَلْ وَجَدْتُمْ ما وَعَدَ رَبُّكُمْ حَقًّا قالوا نَعَمْ﴾ — "So have you found what your Lord promised to be true? They will say: Yes." A positive question (وَجَدْتُمْ), answered نَعَمْ, confirms the positive: they DID find it true.
>
> أَسافَرَ خالِدٌ؟ — نَعَمْ — "Did Khalid travel? — Yes" [i.e. the travel did happen]. The question was phrased positively (أَسافَرَ), so نَعَمْ confirms the positive.
>
> أَما سافَرَ خالِدٌ؟ — نَعَمْ — "Did Khalid NOT travel? — Yes" [i.e. correct, the travel did NOT happen]. The question here was phrased negatively (أَما...), so نَعَمْ this time confirms the NEGATIVE — "yes, that's right, he did not travel."

> **Careful Box**
>
> This is the single most important nuance of نَعَمْ: it never flips the polarity of the question — it always agrees with whatever the question stated, positive or negative. Answering a negatively-phrased question with نَعَمْ does NOT mean "yes, [the opposite happened]" — it means "yes, [you are correct that it did not happen]." Always check the question's own polarity before interpreting a نَعَمْ answer.

## الثاني: بَلٰى

> **Rule Box**
>
> بَلٰى تأتي بَعْدَ النَّفْيِ فَقَط، وَهي لِإيجابِ المَنْفِيِّ — سَواءً كانَ ما قَبْلَها اسْتِفْهامًا أَوْ خَبَرًا.

Unlike نَعَمْ, بَلٰى can ONLY follow something NEGATIVE — and its entire purpose is to FLIP that negative into a positive. It always means "no [you're wrong] — actually, the opposite is true."

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿قالَ أَوَلَمْ تُؤْمِنْ قالَ بَلٰى﴾ — "He [Allah] said: Do you not believe? He [Ibrahim] said: Yes indeed [I believe]." The question was negative (أَوَلَمْ تُؤْمِنْ — "do you NOT believe"), and بَلٰى flips it: "Yes, [in fact] I DO believe."
>
> **بعد استفهام منفي:** ﴿أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ قالوا بَلٰى﴾ — "Am I not your Lord? They said: Yes indeed [You are]." أَيْ أَنْتَ رَبُّنا — meaning, "You are indeed our Lord" — بَلٰى flips the negative question into a positive answer.
>
> **بعد خبر منفي:** لَمْ يَقُمْ زَيْدٌ، قُلْتُ بَلٰى — "Zayd did not stand [someone claims]. I said: Yes indeed [he did]." أَيْ قَدْ قامَ — meaning "he did in fact stand" — here بَلٰى corrects a negative STATEMENT (خَبَر), not a question, showing بَلٰى works after either type of negative.

> **Careful Box**
>
> نَعَمْ and بَلٰى are easy to confuse because both can look like "yes" in translation, but they behave oppositely with negative questions. أَما سافَرَ خالِدٌ؟ answered نَعَمْ means "yes, he indeed did NOT travel" (agreeing with the negative). The same question answered بَلٰى would instead mean "no — actually, he DID travel" (flipping the negative). Which one is meant changes the entire answer's meaning, so context always determines which particle is being used.

## الثالث: إي

> **Rule Box**
>
> إي حَرْفُ إيجابٍ، وَلا يَأْتي إِلّا قَبْلَ القَسَمِ.

إي is a straightforward "yes," but it comes with a strict structural requirement: it is always immediately followed by a قَسَم (an oath) — it is never used as a bare, standalone "yes."

> **Example Box**
>
> ﴿وَيَسْتَنْبِئونَكَ أَحَقٌّ هُوَ قُلْ إي وَرَبّي إِنَّهُ لَحَقٌّ﴾ — "And they ask you: Is it true? Say: Yes, by my Lord, it is indeed true." إي is immediately followed by the oath وَرَبّي ("by my Lord") — this pairing with a قَسَم is obligatory whenever إي is used.

## الرابع: أَجَلْ

> **Rule Box**
>
> أَجَلْ تَأْتي بَعْدَ الخَبَرِ (لا بَعْدَ الاستفهام)، وَتُفيدُ المُوافَقَةَ على ما قيلَ.

أَجَلْ agrees with a preceding STATEMENT (خَبَر) — it is not used to answer a question the way نَعَمْ or بَلٰى are; it signals "yes, I agree with what you just said."

> **Example Box**
>
> نُحِبُّ الأَنْصارَ — أَجَلْ — "We love the Ansar. — Indeed [I agree]." The first speaker made a STATEMENT, not a question; أَجَلْ confirms agreement with that statement.

## الخامس: إِنَّهْ

> **Rule Box**
>
> إِنَّهْ حَرْفُ إيجابٍ نادِرُ الِاسْتِعْمالِ، يَأْتي جَوابًا عَنِ اسْتِفْهامٍ.

إِنَّهْ functions as "yes" in reply to a question, though it is comparatively rare in usage.

> **Example Box**
>
> هَلْ تُحِبُّ الصَّحابَةَ؟ — إِنَّهْ — "Do you love the Companions? — Indeed [yes]."

## السادس: جَيْرِ

> **Rule Box**
>
> جَيْرِ حَرْفُ إيجابٍ، وَهو الأَقَلُّ اسْتِعْمالًا مِنَ السِّتَّةِ، وَيَأْتي جَوابًا مُؤَكَّدًا.

جَيْرِ is the rarest of the six — used only occasionally — but it still functions as an emphatic affirmative response.

> **Example Box**
>
> هَلْ تَتَّبِعُ الرَّسولَ وَأَصْحابَهُ؟ — جَيْرِ لَأَتَّبِعَنَّهُمْ — "Will you follow the Messenger ﷺ and his Companions? — Yes indeed, I will most certainly follow them."

> **Careful Box**
>
> In everyday and Qur'anic usage, نَعَمْ and بَلٰى are by far the most common of the six. إي، أَجَلْ، إِنَّهْ، and جَيْرِ are all real and attested, but comparatively rare — worth knowing for reading classical texts, but not the words you would expect to hear or use as often in ordinary speech.
